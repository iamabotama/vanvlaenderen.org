# Research Thread Handoff — The Post-1545 Van Vlaenderen-Praet Generation

**Thread type:** Active research question, pursued separately from the main Vredius validation work
**Status:** Open — evidence gathered, parentage unresolved, further archival work required
**Originated:** 19 April 2026, during Batch 3 prep for the Vredius validation record
**Handed off to:** Separate research thread for pursuit; this document is the entry point

---

## Executive summary

Vredius (1643) and his Grimarez extract report the legitimate Van Vlaenderen-Praet male line as extinct at the death of Jan II van Vlaenderen on 10 December 1545 ("*mourut, sans generation, avant son pere*"). His father Lodewijk IV van Vlaenderen / Louis of Praet died in 1555 without male issue named in Vredius's text.

**This is incomplete.** An entry in the Huygens Institute's *Digitaal Vrouwenlexicon* on Catharina van Boetzelaer establishes a post-Vredius generation:

- **Jacob van Vlaanderen**, vrijheer van Praet, heer van Beveren en Onlede, married Catharina van Boetzelaer in 1552, died before 1566
- At least one son was born of this marriage, named **Lodewijk van Vlaanderen**, who was still a minor in 1567 when he went into exile with his mother following the Reformation-era troubles

The son Lodewijk — a direct-line Van Vlaenderen male, fathered by a Praet-line Van Vlaenderen, carrying the surname, in exile from Flanders during the 1566–1567 persecutions — has a profile that directly matches the bridge-candidate question the broader *Lions of Flanders* project has been investigating: namely, whether any Van Vlaenderen line that continues the surname into the 17th–18th century parish-register period can be identified from the noble-bastard cohort descended from Louis II de Male.

**Core research questions:**
1. **Who are Jacob van Vlaanderen's parents?** The Vredius text offers several candidates (son of Lodewijk II, grandson via Jean d. 1523, grandson via Josse d. after 1526, or an additional unmentioned son of Lodewijk IV), but does not resolve the question.
2. **What happened to Catharina van Boetzelaer's son Lodewijk van Vlaanderen after 1567?** If he survived, did he reproduce? Where? Under what name? Can any modern Van Vlaenderen descent be traced back to him?
3. **Are there other figures from the same generation** (e.g., "Philips van Praet" mentioned by Jacobus Meyerus as student alongside Jacob) who also need documenting?

**Why this matters to the broader project:** The *Lions of Flanders* site currently states that the legitimate Praet male line ended in 1545. If Jacob and his son Lodewijk (plus a potential Philips) are real and traceable, the site's narrative needs significant revision. More importantly, this may be the project's strongest candidate yet for the bridge question.

## Current evidence inventory

### 1. The Huygens Vrouwenlexicon entry on Catharina van Boetzelaer

**Source:** Huygens Institute for Dutch History (Huygens ING). *Digitaal Vrouwenlexicon van Nederland*. Entry: Boetzelaer, Catharina van (den) (?-na 1567).

**URL:** <https://resources.huygens.knaw.nl/vrouwenlexicon/lemmata/data/CatharinavanBoetzelaer>

**Key extract:**

> BOETZELAER, Catharina van (den), ook bekend als Madame de Praet (gest. na 3-1567), pleitbezorgster van het protestantisme. Dochter van Wessel van (den) Boetzelaer (gest. 1575) en Francine Lodewijksdr. van Praet van Moerkerken (gest. 1562). Catharina van Boetzelaer trouwde (1) in 1552 met **Jacob van Vlaanderen, vrijheer van Praet, heer van Beveren en Onlede (gest. voor 1566)**; (2) in 1566 of 1567 met Frans van Haeften (gest. 1573). **Uit huwelijk (1) is ten minste 1 zoon geboren.**

Relevant facts established by this source:

- Catharina van Boetzelaer's first husband was Jacob van Vlaanderen
- Jacob held the vrijheerlijkheid van Praet (free-lordship of Praet) plus the lordships of Beveren and Onlede
- Marriage was in 1552
- Jacob died before 1566
- The marriage produced **at least one son**
- After Jacob's death, Catharina married Frans van Haeften in 1566 or 1567

**Additional extracts on the son and on the family's Reformation-era activity:**

> In 1552 betrok zij als echtgenote van Jacob van Vlaanderen van Praet het Vrijhof in Aalter bij Brugge.

> In 1561 verbood zij de pastoor van Aalter op Allerzielen de klokken te luiden, en in 1564-1565 was haar kasteel een trefpunt voor protestantse edelen.

> **De vrouwe van Praet nam daarbij alleen haar onmondige zoon Lodewijk van Vlaanderen mee.** In december 1567 werd haar zaak en die van ruim dertig plaatsgenoten door de procureur des konings voor de Raad gebracht.

> Zo werd 'jkvr. Catharina van Boetzelaer, weduwe van wijlen de heer van Praet' in februari 1568 bij verstek veroordeeld tot eeuwige verbanning en verbeurdverklaring van haar goederen.

So the son is named **Lodewijk van Vlaanderen**, was still a minor ("onmondige") in 1567, and was taken into exile by his mother. Their destination was likely Emden (then the center of German Calvinism) or the Palatinate. Both mother and her second husband were cited before the Raad van Beroerten (Council of Troubles) in December 1567 and convicted *in absentia* in February 1568, with eternal banishment and confiscation of goods.

**Huygens's cited sources include:**
- Decavele (1969) — for the Reformation-era Aalter Vrijhof activity
- Verheyden (517–524) — for the February 1568 Raad van Beroerten conviction details
- Fruin (419–420) — for intercepted correspondence in Spanish hands after the Battle of Oosterweel (13 March 1567)
- Des Tombe (191) — Boetzelaer family history
- Bakhuizen van den Brink (112) — 19th-century historical assessment

Huygens is the Dutch national humanities research institute; this is an academically-vetted biographical source, not an amateur compilation.

### 2. The Jacobus Meyerus tutoring reference

**Source:** Wikipedia (Dutch). Jacobus Meyerus (1491–1552), Flemish humanist historian.

**URL:** <https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Jacobus_Meyerus>

**Key extract:**

> Na korte tijd werd hij kapelaan van Sint-Donaas en leidde hij een school in Brugge waar hij onder meer als leerlingen **Jacob en Philips van Praet** had, zonen van **de heer van Praet, Lodewijk van Vlaanderen**.

So Jacobus Meyerus, chaplain of St. Donaas and schoolmaster in Bruges, had "Jacob and Philips van Praet" as students — described as sons of "the Lord of Praet, Lodewijk van Vlaanderen."

**Timing:** Meyerus was active as a schoolmaster in Bruges in the period roughly 1530–1552. The students would have been adolescent during this window. That places Jacob's birth somewhere in the range 1515–1540, consistent with his 1552 marriage to Catharina van Boetzelaer.

**Interpretation:** The "Lodewijk van Vlaanderen" identified as the father here is almost certainly **Lodewijk IV** (d. 1555) — he's the only Lodewijk van Vlaanderen "heer van Praet" active as a father figure in the 1530s–1540s window.

**Consequence:** If Meyerus's identification is accurate, then **Lodewijk IV had at least two sons that Vredius / Grimarez did not mention**: Jacob and Philips. This directly contradicts Grimarez's clean "sans generation" framing for Lodewijk IV's male line (via Jan II).

**Caveat:** Wikipedia is not authoritative. The Meyerus attribution needs to be verified against a primary biography of Meyerus or against his own collected writings.

### 3. Post-Vredius attestations of Van Vlaenderen as Heer van Beveren / Onlede

**Source A:** Wikipedia (Dutch). Beveren (Roeselare).

**URL:** <https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Beveren_(Roeselare)>

**Key extract:**

> Als wapen voert het dorp Beveren het schild van Jan van Vlaanderen.

The village of Beveren still bears the arms of Jan van Vlaanderen. Jan here is almost certainly Jean de Flandre d. 6 September 1523 at Beveren bij Roeselare, the Heer van Onlede en Beveren whose tomb is transcribed in Vredius Tabula XVI p. 280.

**Source B:** City of Roeselare municipal history.

**URL:** <https://www.roeselare.be/stad-en-bestuur/feiten-en-cijfers/beveren>

**Key extract:**

> De beroemdste zoon van het dorp is Jan van Vlaanderen, heer van Beveren en Onlede, bastaardzoon van graaf Lodewijk van Male. Jan Beveren en zijn vrouw Margaretha Boulangère kregen een laatste rustplaats in de kerk van Beveren.

This local history identifies Jan van Vlaanderen as "bastaardzoon van graaf Lodewijk van Male." Chronologically impossible (Louis de Male died 1384, Jan died 1523 = 139 years apart), so the "direct bastard" claim is loose language rather than genealogical fact — likely "descendant of a bastard line of Louis de Male" rather than "direct bastard son." The attribution is useful as corroboration of Jan's presence at Beveren + Onlede; not useful for parentage.

**Source C:** Inventaris Onroerend Erfgoed. Beveren (municipality).

**URL:** <https://inventaris.onroerenderfgoed.be/themas/13725>

**Key extract:**

> Enkele heren waren Jan van Vlaanderen (+ 1523), hoogbaljuw van het Brugse Vrije, wiens familiegrafsteen zich tot 1882 op het Beverense kerkhof bevond en nu bewaard wordt in het Brugse Steenmuseum.

So Jan van Vlaenderen (d. 1523) was **High-Bailiff of the Brugse Vrije**, and his family tombstone stood at Beveren cemetery until 1882, when it was transferred to the **Bruges Stone Museum** ("Brugse Steenmuseum" — probably the Gruuthuse Museum per the Roeselare city source).

**Note:** This source doesn't cover the post-1523 generation or the Jacob/Catharina episode. The ownership transfers described at Beveren go from Van Vlaenderen (up to an unspecified date) → Van Vlaenderen (→ 1523) → the 18th century de Beer family → 1783 the Plotho family. There's no record in this municipal-heritage source of a Van-Vlaenderen Jacob holding Beveren in the 1552–1566 window. Either Jacob held it briefly and the source elides the detail, or the Huygens attribution of Beveren and Onlede to Jacob needs further verification.

### 4. The separate Praet-van-Moerkerken line (Catharina's maternal side)

**Source:** Wikipedia (Dutch). Lodewijk van Praet van Moerkerken.

**URL:** <https://nl.wikipedia.org/wiki/Lodewijk_van_Praet_van_Moerkerken>

**Key extracts:**

> Lodewijk van Praet van Moerkerken ridder, heer van Carnisse en kastelein te Schoonhoven en heer van Cranendonck en Eindhoven (Schoonhoven, 1471 - 1537), was de zoon van Frank van Praet heer van Moerkerke en Merwede (1440-1473) en Elisabeth van Loon (1450-1514).

> Lodewijk trouwde op 5 mei 1497 met Catharina bastaarddochter van Egmond, dochter van Frederik van Egmont.

> Uit het huwelijk van Lodewijk en Catharina zijn de volgende kinderen geboren:
> - **Françoise van Praet** (ca. 1505 - 6 oktober 1562), vrouwe van Moerkerken en Carnisse. **Zij trouwde in 1519 met Wessel VI van den Boetzelaer**
> - François van Praet (Mijnsheerenland, 1506 - 1535)

**This settles an outstanding project question.** The figure currently documented on the *Lions of Flanders* site as "Francoise van Praet van Moerkerke (fl. c. 1519)" who married Wessel van Boetzelaer — and whose generational placement in the Van Vlaenderen-Praet line has been noted as uncertain — is **not** in the Van Vlaenderen-Praet line at all. She descends from the separate **Praet-van-Moerkerken** branch, through Frank van Praet (1440–1473) → Lodewijk van Praet van Moerkerken (1471–1537) → Françoise van Praet (1505–1562).

The Van Vlaenderen-Praet and Praet-van-Moerkerken lines are related through the Praet-Moerkercke marriage of Lodewijk IV + Jossine van Praet (1516), which brought Jossine's maternal inheritance (Praet-Moerkercke via her mother Paschijne van Halewijn) into the Van Vlaenderen-Praet line. But Francoise van Praet van Moerkerken is a **separate figure** from Jossine — Francoise is from the Netherlands-resident Praet-van-Moerkerken line, a collateral of the same Praet family that Jossine inherited from.

Implication: **the current site entry for Francoise van Praet van Moerkerke needs correction, or should be moved to a "related-lines" section rather than framed as Van Vlaenderen-Praet descent.**

**Further source confirming this separation:** Lauwens (2010), *Verhalen uit de genealogie Van Praet* <https://www.laurentii.be/Verhalen%20uit%20de%20genealogie%20Van%20Praet.pdf>. Explicitly treats the Netherlands-resident Praet-Moerkerken line as a separate branch from the Flemish Van Vlaenderen-Praet line.

### 5. What Vredius itself does and does not say

**What Vredius reports for Lodewijk IV's descendants (Vol 2 Tabula XIX pp. 387–388):**

- Only **Jan II** is named as Lodewijk IV's son
- Jan II predeceased his father, "sans generation, avant son pere, en l'an 1545. le 10. de Decembre"
- The implication (not literally stated, but clearly intended by Grimarez) is that Lodewijk IV had no further male issue

**What Vredius does NOT say:**

- Vredius does not say that Lodewijk IV had no other children
- Vredius does not claim to document the full cadet-branch structure after 1555
- Vredius is working from Grimarez's manuscript, which appears to terminate coverage at Jan II

**Conclusion:** Vredius's silence on post-1555 descendants is consistent with either (a) there being no such descendants, or (b) Grimarez's underlying manuscript simply not covering them. The Huygens entry establishes (b) is the case for Jacob at minimum. The extent of the gap is unknown.

**Cross-reference to Damhouder's six-child list for Lodewijk II (Vredius p. 278):**

> *avec laquelle LOYSE de Bruges il eut Loys, Iean, Iaques, Ioffe, Loyfe, & Iehenne de Flandre.*

This passage names a "Iaques" among Lodewijk II's own six children by Louise de Bruges. Chronological analysis:

- Lodewijk II died 1488
- His children (per Damhouder's list): Loys (Lodewijk III, d. 1488/1490), Iean (Jean de Flandre d. 1523), Iaques, Iofse (Josse de Flandre d. after 1526), Loyfe, Iehenne
- If Iaques = Jacob d. before 1566 (m. Catharina 1552), Iaques would be 60–75+ at his 1552 marriage, which is theoretically possible but unusual
- More likely: Iaques is a different Jacob who died earlier or is otherwise undocumented; the 1552 Jacob is a different figure, possibly grandson of Lodewijk II via one of Iean (Beveren) or Iofse (Onlede) or Iaques himself

## Three hypotheses for Jacob van Vlaanderen's parentage

### Hypothesis A — Jacob is Lodewijk II's son "Iaques" from Damhouder's list

**For:** Directly named in Vredius. Would provide a clean line of lordship inheritance (Praet from Lodewijk III, Beveren from Iean, Onlede cadet, etc.).

**Against:** Requires Jacob to be 60+ years old at his 1552 marriage (born before Lodewijk II's 1488 death, marrying in 1552). Possible but unusual. No documentation of his activities between 1488 and 1552.

**Probability:** Low.

### Hypothesis B — Jacob is Lodewijk II's grandson via Jean de Flandre d. 1523 (Heer van Onlede en Beveren)

**For:** Chronologically clean — Jean d. 1523, his son Jacob born c. 1510–1525, marries 1552, dies before 1566. Would explain inheritance of Beveren and Onlede from his father Jean. The Beveren community-level memory (Inventaris Onroerend Erfgoed; Roeselare local history) remembers Jean d. 1523 but doesn't clearly identify his successor — a son who later inherited Praet itself after Jan II's 1545 death would match.

**Against:** No direct documentation yet found of Jean d. 1523 having a son Jacob. Jean was Grand Bailiff of Bruges, well-documented; Buylaert (2011) probably covers his descent.

**Probability:** Moderate-to-high. Needs Buylaert verification.

### Hypothesis C — Jacob is Lodewijk II's grandson via Josse de Flandre d. after 1526

**For:** Josse inherited Onlede, Beveren, and Wijchuize from his brother Jean d. 1523 per Buylaert [881, 882]. If Josse had a son Jacob, Jacob would inherit the same lordships plus add Praet after 1545. Josse married Martina van Moerkerke, so the Moerkercke tie would be reinforced — relevant to Jacob's marriage at the Aalter Vrijhof (the Praet-Moerkercke-Aalter complex).

**Against:** Josse died "after 1526" — exact date unknown. If he died early after 1526 his son Jacob might be too young to marry in 1552. Buylaert is again the key source.

**Probability:** Moderate. Buylaert verification needed.

### Hypothesis D — Jacob is a previously-unknown son of Lodewijk IV (not mentioned by Vredius / Grimarez)

**For:** Jacobus Meyerus's Wikipedia biography explicitly names "Jacob and Philips van Praet" as students who were sons of Lodewijk van Vlaanderen, Heer van Praet — which most naturally identifies with Lodewijk IV. Would explain Jacob becoming vrijheer van Praet after his father's 1555 death (direct inheritance rather than cadet-branch claim).

**Against:** Contradicts Grimarez's "sans generation" framing for Jan II's death — but Grimarez's phrase applies literally only to Jan II's own childlessness, not necessarily to Lodewijk IV's overall issue.

**Probability:** Moderate. Would need verification through Meyerus's own writings or a biography of Meyerus. Also would need to explain why Vredius missed this.

**Hybrid possibility:** Both (B) and (D) could be partly right — Lodewijk IV had one legitimate son (Jan II d. 1545) who died childless, AND additional natural or legitimized sons (Jacob and Philips) who inherited the cadet lordships and later the vrijheerlijkheid after Jan II's death.

## The son Lodewijk van Vlaanderen — the bridge-candidate question

**What Huygens establishes:**

- Born to Jacob van Vlaanderen and Catharina van Boetzelaer, sometime between 1552 and 1566
- Still a minor ("onmondige") in late 1567 when he went into exile with his mother
- Destination of exile: probably **Emden** (German Calvinist center) or the **Palatinate** — Huygens references both
- Mother was convicted *in absentia* of eternal banishment and confiscation of goods in February 1568

**What Huygens does NOT report:**

- His subsequent fate
- Any marriage or children of his own
- Any return to Flanders

**Research targets for tracing Lodewijk:**

1. **Boetzelaer family archives** — Utrecht Archives or the Netherlands National Archives (Nationaal Archief). The Boetzelaer family was prominent; records likely survive.
2. **Emden church registers** — if exile destination was Emden, Calvinist community records should document his adolescence / early adulthood.
3. **Palatinate exile registers** — alternate destination.
4. **Rijksarchief Gent, Raad van Beroerten records** — the full February 1568 conviction file, if survives, would name Lodewijk as the son taken into exile.
5. **Van Haeften family records** — Catharina's second husband Frans van Haeften was from a known Flemish Reformation family; their collective exile trajectory may be traceable.
6. **Decavele (1969)** — the Huygens-cited source for the Aalter Vrijhof period; may contain further detail on the son.

**Why Lodewijk matters to the Lions of Flanders project:**

The project's central research question is: *can any Van Vlaenderen line that descends from Louis II de Male's bastard cohort be traced forward into the parish-register period (17th–19th centuries)?*

The senior legitimate Van Vlaenderen-Praet line extinguished in 1545 (Jan II childless). The Victor line's male descent exits via Joos (died young at Oostburg) and Margareta (married into d'Erpe) by 1520. The Drincham line's male descent is traced through the mid-15th century but not consistently past it. The other Louis II bastards (Louis Haze, Robert of Elverdinghe) either had no documented surname-bearing descent or had descent that exits quickly.

**Lodewijk van Vlaanderen, son of Jacob and Catharina van Boetzelaer, is the only known surname-bearing Van Vlaenderen direct-line male active in the 1560s–1570s** (ignoring the Drincham collaterals which are a separate thread). If he survived the exile, married, and reproduced, his descendants would have been the documented bridge between the medieval Van Vlaenderen noble cohort and any later surname-bearing figures.

**If** the modern American Van Vlaenderen line traced by the project (through the Bassevelde / Waarschoot / Sleidinge Meetjesland cluster) turns out to descend from this Lodewijk, that would answer the fundamental research question decisively. It would also place the bridge through a Reformation-exile Calvinist line — which would have specific religious, social, and geographic implications (probably a return migration to Catholic Flanders in the 17th century or onward movement into the Protestant Netherlands).

That is a high-value research target.

## Provenance — how this research question emerged

On 19 April 2026, during the preparation of Batch 3 (Drincham line §4) of the Vredius 1643 validation record for the *Lions of Flanders* project, Claude (Opus 4.7) noticed while cross-referencing Jean de Flandre d. 6 September 1523 against the Beveren tomb text that the Beveren tomb identifies Jean's father as "Meßire **Loys** de Flandres, Chevalier, Saigneur de Praet" — not "Meßire Iean de Praet" (which would be Johan I). This meant the public *Lions of Flanders* site and the recently-committed Batch 2 of the research doc had erroneously attributed Jean d. 1523 and his brother Josse d. after 1526 to Johan I rather than to Lodewijk II.

During the subsequent due-diligence web search to verify the Lodewijk-II reading (searching FMG directly failed due to robots.txt), the search pulled the Huygens Vrouwenlexicon entry on Catharina van Boetzelaer. That entry unexpectedly named her first husband as **Jacob van Vlaanderen, vrijheer van Praet, heer van Beveren en Onlede**, married 1552, died before 1566, with at least one son — and identified that son as "Lodewijk van Vlaanderen," taken into exile by his mother in 1567.

The finding is significant because:
- Vredius (1643) and Grimarez's extract had led the project to treat the Van Vlaenderen-Praet legitimate male line as extinct at Jan II's 1545 death
- A Jacob holding the vrijheerlijkheid van Praet in 1552 demonstrates the line continued at least one more generation
- A son named Lodewijk van Vlaanderen carrying the Van Vlaenderen surname and still alive in exile from 1567+ is a previously-unknown direct-line Van Vlaenderen male
- The son has the right generational profile to be the bridge between the medieval noble cohort and later parish-register-era Van Vlaenderen figures

The decision to handle this as a separate research thread (rather than inline in the Vredius validation record) was made to preserve the integrity of the Vredius doc as a primary-source validation artifact and to give the Jacob/son-Lodewijk question the focused attention it deserves. This handoff document was written to capture all evidence found to date and to enable a fresh research thread to pick up cleanly.
