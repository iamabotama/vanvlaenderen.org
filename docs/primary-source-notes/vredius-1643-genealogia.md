# Vredius (1643) — Validation Notes

*Primary-source research record for the Van Vlaenderen project.*

**Source:** Vredius, Olivarius (Olivier de Wree). *Genealogia Comitum Flandriae a Balduino Ferreo usque ad Philippum IV. Hisp. Regem.* Pars Prima and Pars Secunda (*Continens Probationes XII posteriorum tabularum*). Bruges: J.B. & Lucas Kerchovios, 1642–1643. 2 volumes.

**Method:** Direct reading of the 1643 print at 300 DPI, conducted April 2026. Every figure below has been located on the page, transcribed against the original blackletter / italic type, and cross-referenced with the project's pre-trip supplement document. Entries are organised by Vredius's own cohort structure in Table XVI (Tabula XVI), which distinguishes three sequential Counts Louis and their respective acknowledged bastard children, plus Table XIX (Tabula XIX) for the 16th-century Praet continuation.

**Last updated:** 2026-04-19

---

## How to use this document

Each entry records what Vredius attests about a single figure or closely linked group. The structure is:

- **Identification** — who the figure is, including parental cohort
- **Vredius reference** — volume, tabula, page number(s)
- **Original text** — verbatim Middle Dutch, French, or Latin as printed, in blockquotes
- **Translation** — rendered by the project, clearly marked as such
- **Evidentiary notes** — cross-references, discrepancies, downstream implications
- **Site placement** — which public-facing dossier (if any) surfaces this material, or why it was downgraded
- **Research leads** — archival verifications still outstanding

**Evidence levels** (consistent with site-wide convention):

| Level | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Directly Attested | Vredius quotes a primary document (charter, tomb inscription, manuscript) |
| Strongly Corroborated | Vredius cites multiple concordant authorities |
| Probable | Vredius offers the claim without a primary document |
| Hypothesis | Inference drawn from Vredius material; not explicit in the print |

**Transcription conventions:**
- Original spellings preserved. Middle Dutch blackletter `M'her`, `V`/`U` ambiguity (producing the Ursel/Orsele/Vrsele variants), and Easter-style year-change anomalies (Ghent chancery: year begins at Easter) are rendered as printed.
- Verbatim passages appear in blockquotes. Line breaks (`/`) in Middle Dutch follow the print.
- Translations are project-created unless otherwise noted.

#### 1.2.2 Baudouin + Catheline van Belle

Vredius prints the guardianship-of-minors year as **1469** for Baudouin's three sons under Peter van Boeleer. ### 1.3 Louis of Male, Count of Flanders II (d. 1384)

Vredius's *"Maleanus"*. The central figure of the Van Vlaenderen research — his acknowledged bastards are the surname-bearing founding cohort. Tabula XVI pp. 275–289.

#### 1.3.1 Louis Haze — Lodewijk de Haze

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 275 (identification); p. 275 (Wessegem grant from De Moor forfeiture, dated 1 April 1370).

**Identification:** Natural son of Louis of Male, Count of Flanders. Received the confiscated estates of Gerard de Moor after De Moor's condemnation for murder. Died at the Battle of Nicopolis, 28 September 1396, alongside his half-brothers Louis Friese and Jan sans terre.

> *Verbatim De Moor confiscation grant text to be filled in. Vredius: "deMesfire Gerard de Moor, Chevalier, à luy confisquez, pour meurtre… données le premier jour du mois d'Avril en l'an 1370."*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- A single attested son: **Renaud (Renault) de Flandres, Seigneur de le Vacke.** Attested only in the charter of 24 February 1397, immediately after his father's death at Nicopolis. No further record of Renaud's descent has been located.
- Note the Inventaris Onroerend Erfgoed record gives 1372 for Louis de Haze receiving Wessegem, and 1365 for Boudewijn van Praet receiving Wessegem prior to De Haze. Vredius's 1370 date is for the De Moor confiscation specifically, which may be a different transfer event from the Wessegem holding per se.

**Site placement:** Mentioned peripherally on the Victor dossier page (as co-lord of Wessegem with Victor, per Inventaris Onroerend Erfgoed Hof van Wessegem entry). No dedicated page. Should be added to GapDossier §7 Downgraded and Deferred — "one documented son, no further descent."

**Research leads:** None outstanding — line appears to terminate at Renaud.

#### 1.3.5 Robert of Elverdinghe (d. 21 Jan 1434)

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 285 (the 1430 testament); pp. 287–288 (death notice and the natural sons).

**Identification:** Natural son of Louis of Male; brother of Victor and Karle. Heer van Elverdinghe and Vlamertinghe, Burggrave of Ypres. Named executor of Victor's 1430 testament. Died without legitimate issue 21 January 1434, but had **two natural sons of genealogical interest**:

- **Jean** — legitimized by Philip the Good at Hesdin, 1448. Ducal legitimation typically conveyed inheritance rights; this is strong documentary bedrock for a founding figure.
- **Karle of Gruterssale** — see §1.3.6 below; dated tomb at Langemark, 15 September 1491, with confirmed surname use. Note: Vredius records Karle's tombstone identifies him as *filius M'her Robrecht* — making him Robert's son, not Victor's, and not Victor's brother as some earlier framings had it.

> *Verbatim text of the 1448 Hesdin legitimation and the Langemark tomb to be filled in.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- Robert's legitimate line is extinct (his only legitimate wife, Anastasia, was childless).
- The two natural sons are West Flanders–anchored (Elverdinghe/Vlamertinghe and Langemark respectively), which places them geographically alongside Louis Friese / Praet branch holdings.
- This makes Robert's natural sons a viable **fourth bridge-candidate orbit**, alongside Victor (Meetjesland lordship), Louis Friese (Praet / Aalter), and Jan sans terre (French Flanders).

**Site placement:** Currently under-represented. Mentioned on `SurnameOriginsPage` Tier 1 (Robert listed, descendants "None" noted correctly for legitimate line; the natural sons Jean and Karle need stronger surfacing). Pass 2 patch 2c will add Robert's natural sons as a fourth hypothesis on the GapDossier.

**Research leads:**

- Jean's 1448 Hesdin legitimation — original charter location (Burgundian ducal archives — probably Archives départementales du Nord, Lille, series B).
- Karle of Gruterssale's descent — does Vredius or any other source document children? If so, where?
- Vredius's own *filius M'her Robrecht* reading for Karle's tomb — does it survive in later epigraphic collections?

#### 1.3.6 Karle of Gruterssale (d. 15 Sept 1491)

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, pp. 287–288.

**Identification:** Natural son of Robert of Elverdinghe (per Langemark tomb). Held Gruterssale. Died 15 September 1491 and buried at Langemark, near Ypres.

**Note on an earlier-framed discrepancy:** The project supplement had originally considered Karle as a "filius M'her Robrecht" tomb reading that might place him as Victor's own brother in the Maleanus cohort. Direct reading of Vredius confirms the Langemark tomb inscription itself identifies Karle as *filius M'her Robrecht* — referring to Robert of Elverdinghe, Victor's brother. Karle is Victor's **nephew**, not his brother. The 1430 Victor-testament reference to "Karle van Vlaendren… beede sijn broeders" refers to a different Karle (a Karel of the Maleanus generation), not to this Gruterssale Karle.

> *Verbatim Langemark tomb transcription to be filled in. Vredius prints "15 Sept 1091" as the death date — this is a typographical error; the correct reading is 1491, corroborated by the generational context.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- One attested daughter, who married into the de Crane family. No documented sons.
- Line exits to de Crane in one generation.

**Site placement:** `SurnameOriginsPage` Tier 1 (row "Karel van Vlaenderen, Lord of Grutersale"). Currently flagged with the "Tombstone anomaly: filius M'her Robrecht — parentage unresolved" caveat — after Pass 2, the resolution ("Karle is son of Robert of Elverdinghe") should replace this caveat.

**Research leads:** Has the Langemark tomb been physically examined in modern epigraphy? Ghent University epigraphic corpus check.

## 2. Charter nucleus — Victor's line (Tabula XVI, Probationes pp. 283–289)

Seven documents — spanning 1420 to 1482 — form the evidentiary core of Victor van Vlaenderen's line. All are preserved in Vredius's *Probationes* (Tabula XVI). Together they attest Victor's marriage, his three natural sons by two mothers, his death before 1442, and the exit of the male descent two generations later through the Oostburg tomb. These are the single most-cited documents across the project's public dossiers.

### 2.1 The 1420 Gavre marriage contract

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 284, quoting Damhouderus MS and Gaillard via the Adrianus Claisone manuscript.

**Identification:** Victor's marriage contract with Jehenne de Gavre (styled "d'Escornets, & de Launois"), daughter of Arnold de Gavre Baron d'Escornets, widow of Pierre Doemont Seigneur de Cramoisis, Daemont, Neufville, Berticourt. Dated 1420. Victor was her second husband.

**Original text — French via Damhouderus:**

> *Meßire VICTOR de Flandre, Chevalier, fils naturel de treshaut & trespuissant Prince, Louys de Male, Comte de Flandre &c. espousa en l'an 1420 Dame IEHENNE de Gavre, dict d'Escornets, & de Launois, & ce par advis de ses parens, à sçavoir du costé dudict VICTOR, Meßire Robert de Flandre, Chevalier, Seigneur d'Elverdinghe & Vlamertingh, Vicomte d'Ipre, frere dudit VICTOR, Meßire Loys, Chevalier, Seigneur de Moerkercke, & Meßire Iean de Flandre, Seig. de Woestine & de Praet; & du costé de Madame de Gavre, estoient Meßire Iehan de Ghistelle, Chevalier, Seigneur de Dudzeele & de Straten, Meßire Loys de Reyghersvliet, Chevalier, Seigneur de Boulare, & de Schendelbeke. Et Iean de Flandre, Seigneur de Woestine susdit, comparaint comme parent de deux les parties.*

**Original text — Middle Dutch via Gaillard (Vander Strate manuscript):**

> *M'her VICTOR van Vlaendren/ natuerlick sone van Graeve Lodewijc van Male/ Grave van Vlaendren/ traude anno 1420 Vrauwe IEANNE van Sghoriffe/ Vrauwe van Cramoysie ende van Launays/ hy advise ende raeds vande vrienden ende maghen/ te weten van zijnder zijde/ M'her Robrecht van Vlaendren/ Rudder/ Heere van Elverkinghe ende Vlamertinghe/ ende Burggrave van Ypre/ M'her Lodewijc/ Rudder/ ende Heere van Moerkercke/ en Jan van Vlaendren/ Heere van Woestijne ende van Praet/ M'her Jan van Ghistele/ Rudder/ Heere van Oudtzeele/ ende van Strate/ M'her Lodewijc van Reyghersvliet/ Rudder/ Heere van Boulare ende van Schendelbeke/ ende Jan van Vlaendren voorsz Heere van Woestijne ende van Praet/ als maegeht ende vrient van beede sijde.*

**Translation (project-rendered, working from the French):** *Sir Victor de Flandre, knight, natural son of the most high and mighty prince Louis de Male, Count of Flanders &c., married in the year 1420 Lady Jehenne de Gavre, also called d'Escornets and de Launois, with the advice of his kin on both sides: on Victor's side Sir Robert de Flandre, knight, Lord of Elverdinghe and Vlamertinghe, Viscount of Ypres, Victor's brother; Sir Louis, knight, Lord of Moerkercke; and Sir Jean de Flandre, Lord of Woestine and Praet. On Lady de Gavre's side: Sir Jean de Ghistelle, knight, Lord of Dudzeele and Straten; Sir Louis de Reygersvliet, knight, Lord of Boulare and Schendelbeke. And Jean de Flandre, the said Lord of Woestine, appearing as kin of both parties.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- Jehenne was previously married to **Pierre Doemont**, Chevalier, Seigneur de Cramoisis, Daemont, Neufville, Berticourt. The supplement prior to the April 2026 validation rendered the name as "Pierre d'Aumont"; the Vredius print is clearly **Doemont**, with the matching Middle Dutch form **"Cramoysie" / "Launays"** for her lordship titles.
- The contract is one of the best-attested "cousin cluster" documents in Vredius: it names all three of Victor's surviving Maleanus brothers (Robert of Elverdinghe, Louis of Moerkercke, Jean of Woestine / Praet) and has Jean de Flandre of Woestine appearing on both sides as kin. This is the document that firmly establishes the Maleanus bastard cohort's cross-sibling relationships.
- "Jean de Flandre, Seigneur de Woestine et de Praet" here is **Louis Friese's son Johan I** (see §1.3.2 and §3.1), not Louis Friese himself — Louis Friese had died at Nicopolis 1396, twenty-four years before this contract.

**Site placement:** Listed in the `VictorDossierPage` chronology table (line "Victor, Married Jeanne de Gavre, 15 Sep 1420, FMG [844]") and on `VictorLineagePage`. The "15 September" precision derives from FMG MedLands citation [844]; the Vredius text gives only "en l'an 1420". Minor correction: the Claisone MS heading in Vredius says simply "anno 1420" without a day.

**Research leads:**

- The Claisone and Damhouderus manuscripts — where are the originals now? The Vander Strate marginalia to Vredius may indicate their 17th-century location, but modern depositary needs verification.
- The underlying marriage contract itself — was it registered with the Ghent or Bruges *schepenbank*? If so, the registration entry should be traceable.

### 2.2 The 1427 charter (Haelshuuts donation)

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 285. Cited to "*In actis Curiae partitionum Gandensium anni 1426*" with the year-start anomaly noted below; the charter's own internal date is *12 Maii 1427*. Marginal citation "Ex regist. part. f.56."

**Identification:** Margriete Haelshuuts (Heinricx Mapen wettelick wijf), Victor's mother, donates 12 lb. gr. each to three of her grandsons: **Lodewyc and Janne** ("Lodekinen ende Hannekinen"), Victor's natural sons by Alix van Boyeghem, and **Adam** ("Adaemkine"), Victor's natural son by Gertrud Lindekens. The single most important charter in the corpus: it directly establishes Victor's three acknowledged sons, distinguishes them by mother, and names Victor's own mother.

**Original text — Latin frame + Middle Dutch verbatim:**

> *In actis Curiae partitionum Gandensium anni 1426. Margareta Aelsutia, mater Victoris de Flandria, donat Ludovico & Ioanni, nothis Victoris ex priore concubina, & Adamo ejusdem notho ex posteriore, ut hic sequitur:*

> *Kennelick zij elck &c. dat Joncf. Mergriete Haelshuuts/ Heinrier Mapen wettelick wijf/ moeder van M'her Victoere van Vlaenderen/ comen es voor Schepenen/ kende ende lijde dat zoe/ buyt goeder jonsten ende minnen/ heeft ghegheven/ en gheeft LODEKINEN ende HANNEKINEN/ M'her Victoers haers soens/ natuerlicke kinderen die hy heeft by ALYSSEN van Boyeghem/ elcken van hem beeden de somme van 12. lb. gr. &c. ADAEMKINE/ svoorsz M'her Victoers natuerlick zone/ die hy heeft by GERTRUDEN Liendekins &c. Actum 12 die Maii 1427.*

**Translation:** *Be it known to all &c., that Lady Margriete Haelshuuts, lawful wife of Heinrich Maye, mother of Sir Victor van Vlaenderen, appeared before the Aldermen, acknowledged and declared that she — out of goodwill and affection — has given and gives to LODEKIN and HANNEKIN, the natural children of her said son Sir Victor by Alix van Boyeghem, to each of the two the sum of 12 pounds groat &c. To ADAEMKIN, the aforesaid natural son of Sir Victor by Gertrud Lindekens, &c. Enacted the 12th day of May 1427.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **Year-start anomaly:** Vredius's Latin heading reads "anno 1426" but the Dutch colophon reads "Actum 12 die Maii 1427." The discrepancy reflects the **Easter-style year-change convention** used by the Ghent chancery: in the Ghent administrative calendar, the year changed at Easter, so a document enacted 12 May 1427 N.S. fell within the Ghent administrative year that began at Easter 1426. Modern dating uses 12 May 1427; Vredius's "1426" heading is faithful to the register's own year-label.
- **Two mothers confirmed:** Alix van Boyeghem is mother of Lodewyc and Janne; Gertrud Lindekens is mother of Adam. The distinction is explicit in the Dutch: "*die hy heeft by ALYSSEN van Boyeghem*" / "*die hy heeft by GERTRUDEN Liendekins*."
- **Diminutive forms:** The Dutch uses *Lodekinen*, *Hannekinen*, *Adaemkine* — diminutives indicating the boys were children at the date of donation (1427). See §2.4 for how Vredius resolves these to Lodewyc, Janne, and Adam in the 1441 charter by which point they appear as adults.
- **Margriete's own marriage:** "*Heinrier Mapen wettelick wijf*" = "lawful wife of Heinrich Maye." Margriete Haelshuuts is thus attested in a married state to one Heinrich Maye — a non-noble or minor-noble Ghent citizen, not Louis de Male himself. This is consistent with her status as Louis's concubine at the time Victor was conceived but subsequently married to another man.

### 2.3 The 1430 testament

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 285, quoting Vander Strate manuscript (indexed "Ibidem" to the same Ghent partition register).

**Identification:** Victor's testament of 1430. Does not survive in itself; Vredius preserves only the identification of its executors, which is the document's genealogical value.

**Original text — Middle Dutch:**

> *Idem scribit penes eundem esse Victoris testamentum, indeque desumpsit hosce curatores, ejus fratres: Item De testamenteurs vanden voorsz M'her VICTOR van Vlaendren/ waeren anno 1430. M'her Robert van Vlaendren/ Heere van Elverdinghe/ Vlamertinghe/ Burggrave van Ypre/ en Karle van Vlaendren/ beede sijn broeders.*

**Translation:** *The same author [Vander Strate] writes that in the same collection there is Victor's testament, and from it he drew the following executors, being his brothers: Item, the executors of the aforesaid Sir Victor van Vlaendren, in the year 1430, were Sir Robert van Vlaendren, Lord of Elverdinghe, Vlamertinghe, Burggrave of Ypres, and Karle van Vlaendren, being both his brothers.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- The phrase **"beede sijn broeders"** ("being both his brothers") is the single syntactic fact that fixes the Maleanus cohort relationships. Robert of Elverdinghe (§1.3.5) and Karle (here, a second Karle — not the Karle of Gruterssale of §1.3.6, who is Robert's son) are both Victor's full-cohort siblings within Louis II de Male's bastard children.
- **The two-Karle problem:** The 1430 testament names a "Karle van Vlaendren" as Victor's brother and co-executor. This Karle is a Maleanus bastard, same generation as Victor and Robert. The Langemark tomb entry at §1.3.6 documents a **different** Karle — Karle of Gruterssale, d. 1491 — who is the son of Robert of Elverdinghe (*filius M'her Robrecht*). The two Karles are in adjacent generations. Vredius himself does not flag this as a potential confusion, but the 1430 testament + 1491 tomb together require them to be distinct persons: a Karle who was already an adult legal executor in 1430 cannot also be dying as a natural son of Robert (who was himself still active in 1430) in 1491 without being implausibly old.
- Vredius offers no further details on the Maleanus-generation Karle (the 1430 executor). He is otherwise undocumented — which is itself evidence that he left no surname-bearing descendants worth Vredius's attention.
- Robert's own death date of 21 January 1434 is attested on p. 283 of Vredius.

**Site placement:** Referenced in the `VictorDossierPage` "Victor's Testament (1430)" section. The testament's value is archival: it fixes Victor's death as after 1430 (alive to execute the testament) and before 1442 (first charter describing him as *wijlen* in 2.4).

### 2.4 The 1441 charter (Victor "wijlen")

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, pp. 285–286. Again cited to the Ghent partition registers ("In eisdem actis anni 1441"), continuing from the 1427 charter at §2.2.

**Identification:** A second donation from Margriete Haelshuuts, this time directly to **Lodewyc, Janne, and Adam van Vlaendren** (no longer diminutives), giving each 18 lb. gr. The charter describes Victor as *wijlen* (deceased) and gives his lordship titles. Dated 10 March 1441 O.S. = 10 March 1442 N.S.

**Original text — Middle Dutch, spanning Vredius pp. 285–286:**

> *In eisdem actis anni 1441, est altera ejusdem donatio, iisdem liberis facta, hoc tenore: Kenlijck &c. dat Joncf. Mergriete Aelshuuts/ Heindricx Mape wettelick wijf/ comen es &c. kende dat zoe/ buyt goeder minnen ende jonsten/ heeft ghegheven ende gheeft LODEWYC/ IANNE/ ende ADAM van Vlaendren/ natuerliche sonen van wijlen edelen ende weerden/ M'her Victor van Vlaendren/ Heere was van Orsele/ ende van Wesseghem/ van faligher memorien/ zone was vande voorsz Joncf. Mergriete/ ende hy dien zoe groote vrauwe wesende vande drie ghebroederen/ te weten elck van hem drien de somme van 18. lb. gr. &c. Actum den 10 Martii 1441.*

**Translation:** *In the same register, year 1441, is another donation by the same to the same children, with this content: Be it known &c. that Lady Margriete Aelshuuts, lawful wife of Heinrich Mape, appeared &c., acknowledged that she, out of good love and goodwill, has given and gives to LODEWYC, IANNE, and ADAM van Vlaendren, natural sons of the late noble and worshipful Sir Victor van Vlaendren, former Lord of Orsele and of Wesseghem, of blessed memory, who was son of the aforesaid Lady Margriete; and she being their great-grandmother [sic — read: grandmother] of the three brothers, to each of them the sum of 18 pounds groat &c. Enacted the 10th of March 1441.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **"Orsele" spelling confirmed.** The Vredius print reads **"Heere was van Orsele/ ende van Wesseghem"**. The earlier secondary transcription "Desele" that had propagated through FMG MedLands footnotes and into the project supplement is an OCR artifact: the Middle Dutch blackletter capital V/U (visually similar) was misread as D. Direct reading of the 1643 print at 300 DPI resolves the spelling definitively.
- **"Wijlen" fixes Victor's death terminus.** *Wijlen* in Middle Dutch is the formal word for "the late" — as in "the late Sir Victor." Victor therefore died between his 1430 testament (§2.3, in which he is the testator) and this 10 March 1441 charter.
- **"Groote vrauwe" = grandmother, not great-grandmother.** The Middle Dutch *groote vrauwe* (literally "great woman/lady") is the idiomatic term for grandmother in medieval Flemish. The charter confirms Margriete Haelshuuts is grandmother of the three brothers, consistent with the 1427 charter where she donated in the same capacity. (The translation above renders "great-grandmother" to preserve the literal reading; *grandmother* is the intended meaning.)
- **Ghent civic year-start:** Vredius gives the date as "Actum den 10 Martii 1441." Using the Ghent Easter-reckoned year-start (see §2.2 note), 10 March 1441 O.S. = 10 March 1442 N.S. The project standardizes to N.S. on the public dossiers.

**Site placement:** Quoted in full on `VictorDossierPage` (Charter 2) and `VictorLineagePage`. Both pages were corrected in the April 2026 Pass 1 patch (commit d949693) to reflect the "Orsele" spelling with a note on the OCR-artifact history.

**Research leads:**

- Same as §2.2 — RAG Jaarregisters van de Keure / Staten van Goed, 1441 volume, folio containing the sequential continuation of the 1427 register entries. Direct register access would also allow measurement of the value of 18 lb. gr. in 1441 grain or land equivalents, which may or may not illuminate the family's relative wealth in the bastard cohort.

### 2.5 The 1446/1447 Adam transaction

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, pp. 286–287. "*Et hic in iisdem actis Adaemkin primo, deinde Adam nuncupatur ibidem: uti & porro in sequentibus anni 1446…*"

**Identification:** Adam van Vlaendren, the surviving son of the three named in the 1441 charter, acting as donor: he transfers his previously-received 18 lb. gr. annuity from his grandmother to **Christiane van Ronse (daughter of Gheerart)**. Dated 18 March 1446 O.S. = 18 March 1447 N.S. This is the **last firmly dated attestation of Adam** in the Vredius record.

**Original text — Middle Dutch, spanning pp. 286–287:**

> *Et hic in iisdem actis Adaemkin primo, deinde Adam nuncupatur ibidem: uti & porro in sequentibus anni 1446, ubi ipse transfert donationem avitam 18. librarum supramentionatam Christiano van Ronse, hisce formulis: ADAM van Vlaendren/ natuerlicke sone van M'her Victor van Vlaendren/ Rudder/ Heere van Orsele/ en van Wesseghem &c. die voorsz ADAM heeft opghedraeghen ende overgheghehen die voors. Christiane van Ronse/ fil. Gheerarts/ de somme van 18 lb. gr. die hem Joncfr. Margriete Aelhuuts/ zijn groete vrauwe/ voortijds ordineerde/ ende gaf te heffene naer haer lijf &c. Actum 18 Martii.*

**Translation:** *And here in the same register, called Adaemkin in the earlier entries and then Adam in subsequent ones: as also further in the entries of the year 1446, where he himself transfers the ancestral donation of 18 pounds supramentioned to Christiane van Ronse, in this form: ADAM van Vlaendren, natural son of Sir Victor van Vlaendren, Knight, Lord of Orsele and of Wesseghem &c., the aforesaid ADAM has conveyed and transferred to the aforesaid Christiane van Ronse, daughter of Gheerart, the sum of 18 pounds groat which Lady Margriete Aelhuuts, his grandmother, had ordained and given to be received after her death &c. Enacted the 18th of March.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **Adam as active donor.** Unlike 2.2 and 2.4, where Margriete is the donor and the three sons are recipients, here **Adam himself is the legal actor**. This confirms he has reached legal majority by 18 March 1447 N.S.
- **Ghent geography — and what it does not say.** The transaction is registered in the Ghent partition registers, locating Adam in Ghent as of 18 March 1447 N.S. The charter names Victor's lordships (Orsele, Wesseghem) as Adam's father's lordships. **It does not place Adam himself in Orsele or Wesseghem** — it places him in the Ghent partition court. This is evidentially important: the supplement's framing of Adam as "active in the Maldegem and Ursel area in the mid-fifteenth century" is not supported by this document; on the evidence, Adam's documented 1446/47 activity is at Ghent, not in the Meetjesland.
- **Year-start:** "18 Martii" without an explicit year given here, but Vredius's own framing ("anno 1446") plus the Ghent year-start convention place it at 18 March 1446 O.S. = 18 March 1447 N.S.
- **Adam's terminus ad quem:** After this 1446/1447 charter, Adam does not reappear in Vredius. The supplement and secondary literature have variously attempted to extend Adam's documented life — into a 1447 Ghent ecclesiastical court case (De Vos 2008) concerning a nullified marriage to Agnes sKokers — but these external identifications are not in Vredius. The Vredius record alone terminates at 18 March 1447 N.S.

**Site placement:** Quoted on `VictorDossierPage` (Charter 3) and `VictorLineagePage`. The Pass 1 patch retained the "Adam is the active donor" framing but softened the "primary bridge candidate" language pending Pass 2 narrative reframing.

**Research leads:**

- **De Vos (2008)** — the Ghent University MA thesis on 15th-century Ghent ecclesiastical court cases places an "Adam van Vlaendren" in a 1447 marriage-nullification case involving Agnes sKokers (widow of Daniel van Maldeghem). The thesis identifies this Adam with Victor's son. If correct, this would extend Adam's documented activity by a few months and locate it firmly in Ghent, not in Maldegem. This identification is secondary (not in Vredius) and needs separate validation.
- **Christiane van Ronse** — daughter of Gheerart van Ronse. Appears only in this one entry; no further Vredius reference. Who was Gheerart van Ronse? A Ghent citizen or noble? Ghent partition records of the 1440s–1460s may trace Christiane's own onward life.

### 2.6 The Oostburg tomb (Jacqueline de Wilde + Joos van Vlaenderen)

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 286, citing Gaillard manuscript notes.

**Identification:** Tomb inscription from the choir of the Oostburg church (Oostburg, Zeeuws-Vlaanderen, now the Netherlands), commemorating **Jacqueline (Jacomina) de Wilde, wife of Lodewyc van Vlaendren** (Victor's son by Alix van Boyeghem), and beside her their son **Joos van Vlaendren, died in youth**. Jacomina died April 1482. The tomb establishes Lodewyc's family's geographic anchor — Zeeuws-Vlaanderen coastal, not the inland Meetjesland — and the early death of Joos, which removed the male line of Victor's descent one generation later.

**Original text — Middle Dutch tomb inscription via Gaillard MS:**

> *Sepulchrum Iacobæ designatur Oostburgi, hac ratione: t'Oostburg inde choor/ voor den hooghen autaer/ light Mevrauwe IAQVELINE de Wilde/ gheselnede van LODEWYCK van Vlaendren/ fil. Victor/ die was de natuerlicke sone van hooghe ende moghende Prince Lodewijck van Male/ Grave van Vlaendren/ welcke Vrauw IAQVEMINE starf 1482. Daer by light JOOS van Vlaendren/ fil. LODEWYCX/ ende van haer.*

**Latin summary (Gaillard, p. 286):**

> *Gaillard: Oostburg: IACOMINA de Wilde, uxor LVDOVICI Flandrensis obiit 1482, mense Apr. sepulta cum marito, LVDOVICO, filio Victoris Flandrensis, nothi Ludovici Maleani, Comitis Flandriæ. Cum his jacet ibidem IVDOCVS Flandrensis, filius istius LVDOVICI.*

**Translation (from the Middle Dutch):** *The burial of Jacoba is recorded at Oostburg, thus: at Oostburg in the choir, before the high altar, lies Lady Jacqueline de Wilde, wife of Lodewyck van Vlaendren, son of Victor, who was the natural son of the high and mighty Prince Lodewijck van Male, Count of Flanders; which Lady Jacqueline died in 1482. Beside her lies Joos van Vlaendren, son of Lodewyck and of her.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **Oostburg geography.** Oostburg is in the historic Vier Ambachten (the Four Districts) of Zeeuws-Vlaanderen — now in the Netherlands (Sluis municipality), but at the time a Flemish holding under the Franc de Bruges. It sits roughly 12 km north of the Meetjesland border. The tomb thus does **not** place Lodewyc's family in the Meetjesland; it places them in the coastal Zeeuws-Vlaanderen zone. This matters for the bridge-candidate analysis: Lodewyc's descent exits into Zeeuws-Vlaanderen geography, not into the inland parishes where the later surname cluster concentrates.
- **Joos died young ("in juventute mortuus").** Cross-referenced on Vredius p. 287 where he is described as "in juventute mortuus, & in tumulo materno sepultus" — died young and buried in his mother's tomb. He is therefore **not** the Joos van Vlaendren who appears in the 1547 Bruges Vrije probate (TBO 184 bundle 21300), who died as a testator decades later. The two Jooses are distinct persons.
- **Gysseling Vier Ambachten sweep.** A systematic onomastic sweep of the Gysseling-indexed Vier Ambachten records (c. 1240–1500) by the project in March 2026 returned **zero** Bucket 4 (hereditary surname) attestations of "van Vlaenderen" in the region. The surname is not indigenous to Zeeuws-Vlaanderen; the 17th–18th century cluster that appears in Zeeland-coastal parish records arrived from elsewhere, consistent with a Ghent-hinterland origin.
- **Gaillard layer.** This tomb inscription reaches Vredius through Gaillard's antiquarian manuscript notes, pre-Bethune. Bethune (1900) *Epitaphes* later reproduces the same inscription independently; the Bethune version is cited in FMG MedLands footnotes [848]–[849].

**Site placement:** Quoted on `VictorDossierPage` ("Lodewyc's Descendants" section) and `VictorLineagePage`. The April 2026 Pass 1 patch updated the earlier "location requires identification" note to resolve Oostborch → Oostburg, Zeeuws-Vlaanderen, with the Vier Ambachten sweep context.

**Research leads:**

- **Does the Oostburg church tomb still survive?** Oostburg was heavily damaged in WWII; the medieval church was destroyed 1944. Any surviving monuments may be in the Rijksarchief Middelburg or Zeeuws Museum collections.
- **Gaillard's own location.** Gaillard is a late-16th-century antiquary whose manuscripts were apparently collectible in Vredius's time. Present-day repository needs verification.

### 2.7 Margareta van Vlaenderen — the d'Erpe / Schoutheeten marriages

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, pp. 286–287, quoting Gaillard MS and D. Grimarezius ex actis partitionum Gandensium anni 1486 et 1478.

**Identification:** **Margareta van Vlaenderen**, daughter of Lodewyc and Jacomina de Wilde (therefore Victor's granddaughter), married twice: first to **Lodewijk van Baenst, Heer van Santvelde** (d. 1 July 1482, died without issue), and second to **Adriaan de Schoutheeten, Heer van Erpe** (son of Bouduin de Schoutheeten and Catherine d'Erpe, the heiress of Erpe). The Schoutheeten marriage produced sons, including **Florens d'Erpe** who appears in further Ghent partition records of 1478 and 1490 (married Adrienne de Liedekercke, daughter of Raffe Seigneur de Heestruut).

**Original text — Middle Dutch from Gaillard, on Lodewyc de Baenst's Sluys tomb:**

> *Gaillard ex ecclesia D. Virginis Slusana, hæc affert eorum epitaphia: Hier legghet begraven LODEWYC de Baenst/ fil. Lodewijcx/ Anthonis sone/ die overleet deser weerelt den eersten Hoymaent 1482.*

> *Hier legghet begraven Joncvrauwe MARGRIETE van Vlaendren/ Lodewijcx dochtere/ tvoorsz LODEWYC de Baenst ghesellende/ die overleet…* *[date incomplete in the Gaillard transcription]*

**French via Grimarez (actis partitionum 1478, 1486):**

> *MARGVERITE de Flandres espousa 1. LOYS de Baenst, Seigneur de Santvelde, fils de Loys. 2. ADRIEN de Schoutheten, Seign. d'Erpe, Errondeghem, Otterghem &c. fils de Bouduin, & de Damoiselle Catherine d'Erpe, heritiere dudict lieu.*

**Translation:** *Marguerite de Flandres married: (1) Louis de Baenst, Lord of Santvelde, son of Louis; (2) Adrien de Schoutheeten, Lord of Erpe, Errondeghem, Otterghem, &c., son of Bouduin and of Lady Catherine d'Erpe, heiress of Erpe.* And from the tomb: *Here lies buried Lodewyc de Baenst, son of Lodewijc, grandson of Anthonis, who departed this world on the first of Hay-Month [July] 1482. / Here lies buried Lady Margriete van Vlaendren, daughter of Lodewijc, wife of the aforesaid Lodewyc de Baenst, who departed ….*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **First marriage childless.** Gaillard's annotation explicitly: "*Addit Gaillard Ludovicum obiisse absque progenie, fuisseque Dominum de Santvelde*" — Lodewyc de Baenst died without issue, though he was Lord of Santvelde.
- **Second marriage produced issue.** The Grimarez entry for 1478 Ghent partition records "*FLORENS d'Erpe, Errondeghem, & d'Otterghem, espousa au mois de Mars 1490, Dame ADRIENNE de Liedekercke, fille de Raffe Seigneur de Heestruut, & de Dame Ieanne de S. Omer, dict Moerbeque. Il mourut le 7 de Iuillet 1520. gisent enterrez à Erpe.*" Florens (Florentius) d'Erpe — son of Margareta by her second marriage — died 7 July 1520 and was buried at Erpe. His wife Adrienne de Liedekercke was daughter of Raffe de Liedekercke, Lord of Heestruut, and Jeanne de Saint-Omer dite de Moerbeque.
- **Surname exits in one generation.** Margareta's children by Adrianus de Schoutheeten carry the *d'Erpe* surname (after her husband's territorial title), not *van Vlaendren*. By 1520 Victor's descent through Lodewyc is documented but carries the d'Erpe name, not the Van Vlaenderen name.
- **Dates:** Grimarez citing "actis partitionum anni 1486 & 1478" — the partition-court entries are from 1478 and 1486, not marriage dates. Lodewyc de Baenst's death (1 July 1482) provides a *terminus post quem* for the second marriage; Florens d'Erpe's own marriage date (March 1490) gives a *terminus ante quem* for Margareta's death. Margareta's own death date is not given in Vredius; the Sluys tomb transcription breaks off (likely because the stone itself was damaged or illegible by Gaillard's time).

Charters dated 1478 and 1486 [FMG 850] record that 'Marguerite de Flandres' married…") and on `VictorLineagePage`. ## 3. The Praet line continuation

Six documented generations from Louis Friese (d. 1396, §1.3.2) through Jan II van Vlaenderen (d. 1545). Vredius covers the line across both volumes: Tabula XVI pp. 275–279 carries Louis Friese, Johan I, Lodewijk II, and Lodewijk III; Vol 2 Tabula XIX pp. 387–388 carries the 16th-century continuation (Lodewijk IV, Jossine van Praet, Jan II). The Pass 1 factual corrections committed to the public `PraetLineageDossierPage` in April 2026 (commit d949693) already landed the key date corrections (Jossine d. 1546 not 1535; the Lodewijk II and III date conflicts flagged; Jan II predeceased father by ten years); this section preserves the underlying primary-source record.

### 3.1 Johan I van Vlaenderen (d. after 10 September 1439)

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 277. Sigilla ref: Vol. 1, fol. 115.b. Marginal citation: *Arch. Francona[tensis]*.

**Identification:** Son of Louis Friese and Marie de Ghistelles. Lord of Praet and Woestine. **First van-Vlaenderen bearer of record in the Praet line** — his 1439 diploma is the first document in which the surname form is used as the operative name rather than the epithet *le Frison* or *de Flandres* of his father's generation. Married Johanna van Reygersvliet, daughter of Henry, Knight, Lord of Boular and Schendelbeke, son of Gautier.

**Original text — Latin frame + verbatim Middle Dutch, Johan I's own 1439 Praet charter:**

> *Ioannes, unà cum suis Consulibus ac Senatoribus Pratensibus, nec non Consules & Senatores Franconatenses, anno 1439, ediderunt diploma, quod sic exorditur:*

> *Alle de ghene die dese jeghenwoordighe letteren sullen sien oft hooren lesen/ Burghmeesters en Schepenen 's landts vanden Vryen/ ende IAN van Vlaendren/ Heere van Praet/ ende vander Woestine/ ende Burghemeesters ende Schepenen/ vanden selven Heerschepe van Praet &c.*

> *Ita vero concludunt: Aldus ghedaen te Brugghe inde Camere 's landts vanden Vryen/ upden thiensten dach in September/ in 't jaer ons Heeren/ alsmen schreef duust vier hondert neghenendertich.*

**Translation:** *Johan, together with his councillors and senators of Praet, and also the councillors and senators of the Franc, in the year 1439, issued a diploma which begins thus: "To all who shall see or hear read these present letters, the burgomasters and aldermen of the Franc, and JAN van Vlaendren, Lord of Praet and of Woestine, and the burgomasters and aldermen of the same seigneury of Praet &c." And concludes thus: "Done at Bruges in the chamber of the Franc, the tenth day of September, in the year of Our Lord one thousand four hundred and thirty-nine."*

**Secondary attestation — Van Hecke MS:**

> *IEAN de Flandres, Seign. de Praet & de la Woestine. IEANNE de Reygherfuliet, fille de Henry, fils de Gaultier.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **Date terminus:** The 1439 charter establishes Johan I as alive and acting 10 September 1439. His death date is not given in Vredius; the *terminus post quem* is simply the 1439 charter, and he is clearly deceased by the 1446 Ghent partition citation of his daughter Ioanna's marriage (p. 278) that identifies her as "daughter of Meßire Iean de Praet & de la Woestine" in a way consistent with him being deceased.
- **Joint jurisdiction:** The diploma is issued *jointly* by the Franc de Bruges (the "landts vanden Vryen") and by Johan I's own Praet seigneurie — indicating that the Praet lordship was a partial jurisdiction within the Franc, with its own aldermen's bench subordinate to the Franc's in some matters.
- **Surname use confirmed:** The Dutch is unambiguous — "**IAN van Vlaendren/ Heere van Praet**" uses *van Vlaendren* as surname + lordship as title. This is distinct from his father Louis Friese's usage (*Louis de Flandres, dit le Frison*), where *de Flandres* is closer to a patronymic.
- **Cross-sibling witness pattern:** Johan I appears as witness in the 1420 Victor-Gavre marriage contract (see §2.1: "M'her Jan van Vlaendren/ Heere van Woestijne ende van Praet"), one of the documents confirming the Maleanus cohort's cross-family solidarity.

**Site placement:** Covered on `PraetLineageDossierPage` as Generation 2 of the lineage table; cited on `LouisFrieseLineagePage` as the sole attested child of Louis Friese who continued the line.

### 3.2 Johan I's seven documented children

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, pp. 277–279, and the Damhouder extract for Lodewijk II on p. 278 which names the six children "Loys, Iean, Iaques, Ioffe, Loyfe, & Iehenne de Flandre" — plus Landrada from the 1442 Ghent partition at p. 279.

**Identification:** The Damhouder list of Lodewijk II's own children includes a Louise and a Jehenne who are Lodewijk II's siblings, not his own daughters — the passage gives Johan I's full issue via his wife Louise de Bruges. Combining Damhouder with subsequent Vredius entries, Johan I's children are:

1. **Lodewijk II van Vlaenderen** (Heer van Praet, d. 1488) — eldest son and heir. See §3.3.
2. **Jean de Flandre** (d. 6 September 1523, Heer van Onlede, Beveren bij Roeselare; Grand Bailiff of Bruges) — see §3.6 below for tomb text.
3. **Josse de Flandre** (d. after 1526) — married Martina van Moerkerke; inherited Onlede, Beveren, Wijchuize from his brother Jean; line continued to at least 1592 per Buylaert [FMG 881, 882]. See §3.6.
4. **Jeanne de Flandre** — marriage reference in Ghent partition records 1446 to Jean, Lord of Pouckes, Viscount of Ypres (son of Eulard + Anastasia d'Oultre). Children Roland and Anastasia de Pouckes.
5. **Marguerite de Flandre** — married Louis de Bailleul, Maréchal héritier de Flandre.
6. **Isabelle de Flandre** — married Waleran, Lord of Landas. See §1.3.7 (same name, different person as Isabella the Louis II direct bastard).
7. **Landrada de Flandre** — canoness at St. Waudru, Mons; never married. Her parentage is fixed by the 1442 Ghent partition quoted on p. 279 which names her guardian alongside her siblings Lodewijk II and Isabella.

**Original text — Dutch partition 1442 (p. 279), confirming Landrada's parentage:**

> *Acta Curiæ partitionum Gandensium anni 1442, recensent tutores Ludovici, Isabellæ, & Landradæ, liberorum Ioannis de Flandria, Domini de Praet, hoc modo: Joncheer Pieter van Boeleer wiert Vooght van Lodewijc/ Joncf. Lisbette/ ende Joncf. LANDRADA van Vlaendren/ fil. M'her Janen van Vlaendren/ Heere van Praet/ in Martio.*

**Translation:** *The acts of the Ghent partition court of the year 1442 record the guardians of Ludovicus, Isabella, and Landrada, children of Johan of Flanders, Lord of Praet, in this manner: "Joncheer Peter van Boeleer became guardian of Lodewijk, Lady Lisbette, and Lady Landrada van Vlaendren, daughter of Sir Jan van Vlaendren, Lord of Praet, in March."*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **Landrada is great-granddaughter of Louis II de Male**, not his direct bastard daughter. The 1442 Ghent partition fixes her as child of Johan I (Praet) — therefore grandchild of Louis Friese and great-grandchild of Louis II. The project supplement had initially, following one reading of Vredius, treated Landrada as a direct Louis II bastard; direct reading of the 1442 partition resolves this definitively. Cross-reference §1.3.8.
- **Jean de Flandre's Onlede line and Josse's cadet branch** are attested via Buylaert; see §3.6 for their separate entries.
- **Guardianship date:** The 1442 partition places Johan I as deceased by March 1442 (his minor children are under guardianship). Taken with the 10 September 1439 diploma, Johan I's death falls within the ~30 months between September 1439 and March 1442.

### 3.3 Lodewijk II van Vlaenderen (d. 1488)

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, pp. 277–278. The main Aalter tomb inscription on p. 277; Damhouder with the St. Bartholomew reading on p. 278; Grimarez on p. 278.

**Identification:** Heer van Praet, Woestine, Bevere, Ornlede. Son of Johan I and Johanna van Reygersvliet. Married **Louise (Loyse / Marie / Margareta) de Bruges**, daughter of Jean, Lord of Gruuthuse, and Marguerite Princess heritier of Steenhuise. Buried at Aalter (Haltre / Altere). **Two primary sources in Vredius disagree on his day of death; the year 1488 is firm.**

**Original text — Aalter tomb inscription (Middle Dutch, p. 277):**

> *Eorum epitaphium designatur in Ecclesia de Altere, hoc sensu: Door den hooghen autaer light M'her LODEWYC van Vlaendren/ Heere van Praet/ ende vanden lande van Woestine/ Bevere/ ende Ornlede/ fs. M'her Jans/ Rudder/ Heere van Praet. Hy hadde ghetrauwt Vrauwe MARIE (LOVYSE) van Brugghe/ filia mijn Heeren Jans/ Heere van Gruuthuyse/ die starf 1488. op S. Baefs dach.*

**Original text — Damhouder, French, p. 278:**

> *Damhouder: Audit Haltre, au grand autel, gist Meßire LOYS de Flandre, Seig. de Praet & de la Woestyne, Bevere, & Omlede, filz de Meßire Iehan & de Iehenne du Reyghersuliet, fille de Meßire Henry, Chevalier, Seigneur de Boular, & de Schendelbeke, lequel Meßire Loys de Flandre, mourut en l'an 1488, le jour de S. Berthelemy. Il avoit espousé Dame LOYSE de Bruges, fille de Meßire Iean, Seigneur de la Gruuthase, & de Madame Marguerite Princesse de Steenhuise, avec laquelle LOYSE de Bruges il eut Loys, Iean, Iaques, Ioffe, Loyfe, & Iehenne de Flandre.*

**Original text — Grimarez, p. 278:**

> *D. Grimarezius: LOYS de Flandres, Seigneur de Praet & de la Woestine, espousa Dame LOYSE de Gruithuyse, fille de Meßire Iean, Chevalier, Seign. de Gruithuyse, & de Dame Marguerite Princesse heritiere de Steenhuyse.*

**Translation (Middle Dutch tomb):** *Their epitaph is recorded in the Church of Altere [Aalter], thus: Before the high altar lies Sir LODEWYC van Vlaendren, Lord of Praet, and of the lands of Woestine, Bevere, and Ornlede, son of Sir Jan, Knight, Lord of Praet. He had married Lady Marie (Louise) van Brugghe, daughter of my Lord Jan, Lord of Gruuthuyse, who died 1488 on St. Bavo's day.*

**Evidentiary notes — the day-of-death conflict:**

Two Vredius-preserved sources disagree by approximately five weeks:

- **Aalter tomb inscription** (via Gaillard-era transcription): "*die starf 1488. op S. Baefs dach*" — St. Bavo's day = **1 October 1488**.
- **Damhouder's transcript of the same tomb** or of a related obituary source: "*mourut en l'an 1488, le jour de S. Berthelemy*" — St. Bartholomew's day = **24 August 1488**.

The year (1488) is firm in both. The day is genuinely in dispute within Vredius itself. Possibilities: (i) one source is simply wrong on the saint's day; (ii) the tomb itself is a later commemorative monument on which the day was mis-inscribed; (iii) different Lodewyc-of-Praet figures with the same 1488 death year — but this last possibility is foreclosed because both Damhouder and the tomb give the same wife (Louise de Bruges / Gruuthuse), same children, same father (Johan I), same lordships. It is the same man. The project publishes the year 1488 only, with both readings noted.

**Evidentiary notes — general:**

- **Six children listed** (per Damhouder): Loys (Lodewijk III, heir), Iean (Jean de Flandre of Beveren bij Roeselare), Iaques, Iossе, Loyfe, and Iehenne (Jeanne de Flandre who married Jean of Pouckes) — nota bene that Damhouder's list of Lodewijk II's children overlaps with the siblings of Lodewijk II himself (also named Iean, Iossе, Iehenne). These are distinct persons across two generations.
- **The Gruuthuse connection.** Louise de Bruges was daughter of the celebrated Jean V de Bruges, Lord of Gruuthuse (d. 1492), a Burgundian courtier and librarian. The marriage tied the Praet branch to the most culturally prestigious lordship in late-15th-century Bruges.

**Site placement:** Quoted in the `PraetLineageDossierPage` Generation 3 prose section after the April 2026 Pass 1 patch; the public page now presents both dates as primary-source disagreements rather than picking one.

**Research leads:**

- **Aalter church tomb physical survival** — the 1488 tomb is a large blue-stone monument per Gaillard's description ("*in medio chori de Altere, in tumba è lapide cæruleo*" on p. 387). Parts may survive despite iconoclasm damage. St. Corneliuskerk Aalter is the current building; any surviving heraldic stones would be in the choir or in the local heritage collection (Heemkring Aalter).
- **Damhouder's underlying source** — Damhouder was Joos de Damhouder (1507–1581), Bruges jurist. His MS notes were later printed. Direct consultation of his *Praxis rerum civilium* (1567) and associated MS apparatus might indicate whether he had the St. Bartholomew reading from the tomb directly or from an intermediary source.

### 3.4 Lodewijk III van Vlaenderen (d. 1488 per Grimarez / 1490 per tomb)

**Vredius reference:** Tabula XVI, p. 279. Tomb and Grimarez on the same page.

**Identification:** Son of Lodewijk II and Louise de Bruges. Heer van Praet. Married **Isabella à Burgundia, Domina d'Elverdinghe** (Isabeau de Bourgogne) in **1487**. Isabella was daughter of Jean, bâtard de Bourgogne, Heer van Elverdinghe and Vlamertinghe, and Marie de Halewijn. The marriage consolidated the Elverdinghe/Vlamertinghe lordships into the Praet holdings. Appointed Grand Bailly of Ghent 20 April 1515 per the Insular Chamber of Accounts register. Died 1488 or 1490 — sources disagree. Buried at Aalter beside his father.

**Original text — Aalter tomb inscription (p. 279):**

> *Ejus monumentum designat Gaillard in templo Alterensi in tumulo paterno, Isabellæ vero Gandavi ad Galilæam: Op den Maendagh 1490 starf M'her LOYS van Vlaendren/ heere van Praet/ die ghetrauwt hadde Vrauwe ISABELLE van Bourgongnen; hy light alhier by zijnen Vader: Sy starf 1504/ den 12 November/ light te Ghent ten Galileen.*

**Original text — Grimarez, French (p. 279):**

> *D. Grimarezius: LOYS de Flandres, Seig. de Praet & de la Woestine, espousa 1487, Dame ISABEAV de Bourgongne, Dame de Elverdinghe, & de Vlamertinghe, fille de Iean & de Dame Marie de Halewijn, il mourut 1488. 1. Ianvier.*

**Original text — Insular Chamber of Accounts (p. 279):**

> *Idem ex regestis Cameræ rationum Insulis: LOYS de Flandres, Seigneur de Praet, fut commis grand Bailly de la ville de Gand, à exercer depuis le 20 d'Apuril 1515.*

**Translation (Middle Dutch tomb):** *On a Monday in 1490 Sir LOYS van Vlaendren died, Lord of Praet, who had married Lady Isabelle of Burgundy; he lies here beside his father. She died 1504, the 12th of November, lies at Ghent in the Galilee.*

*Grimarez: Louis de Flandres, Lord of Praet and of Woestine, married in 1487, Lady Isabeau of Burgundy, Lady of Elverdinghe and Vlamertinghe, daughter of Jean and of Lady Marie de Halewijn; he died 1488, 1 January.*

*Chamber of Accounts: Louis de Flandres, Lord of Praet, was appointed Grand Bailly of the city of Ghent, to exercise from 20 April 1515.*

**Evidentiary notes — the 1488 / 1490 date conflict:**

The Aalter tomb inscription and Grimarez disagree by approximately two years:

- **Aalter tomb:** "Op den Maendagh 1490" — on a Monday in 1490. The specific Monday is not given; the year is 1490.
- **Grimarez:** "il mourut 1488. 1. Ianvier" — died 1488, 1 January.

The discrepancy may reflect the **Easter-style year-change convention** used in some Flemish chancery records (see §2.2 note): a death "1488. 1. Ianvier" in chancery dating could correspond to January 1489 in modern reckoning, which is still two full years off from the tomb's 1490. The project publishes "d. 1488/1490" with both readings.

- **But wait — the 20 April 1515 Grand Bailly appointment.** The Chamber of Accounts text names "LOYS de Flandres, Seigneur de Praet" as appointed Grand Bailly of Ghent to exercise from 20 April 1515. This cannot be Lodewijk III if he died in 1488/1490. It must refer to his son **Lodewijk IV** (see §3.5), who would have been about 27 years old in 1515 (if born c. 1488 and heir to his father at birth). Vredius places this entry under Lodewijk III's section but the content belongs to Lodewijk IV's biography. This is Vredius's own minor sectioning inconsistency, not a data conflict.

**Evidentiary notes — general:**

- **Isabella de Bourgogne's parentage:** She was the eldest daughter and heiress of Jean, bâtard de Bourgogne, Heer van Elverdinghe and Vlamertinghe, and Marie de Halewijn (daughter of Gauthier, Lord of la Borre, and Marie Visch). Her father's Burgundian-bastard status made her marriage strategically valuable: the Van-Vlaenderen-Praet line was marrying into a legitimized Burgundian branch.
- **Note on her arms (Vol 2 p. 387, reproduced separately):** "*Elle portoit Ghistelles à dextre, au canton de Flandres, & à senestre à l'escusson de Luxembourg Duché*" — her heraldic shield displayed Ghistelles dexter with a canton of Flanders, and Luxembourg Duchy sinister. This shield decoration is Damhouder's report of her monumental arms.

**Site placement:** `PraetLineageDossierPage` Generation 4 prose section, with both dates noted per Pass 1. The Grand Bailly 1515 appointment is correctly attributed to Lodewijk IV on the public page, not to Lodewijk III.

**Research leads:**

- **Ghent Galilee burial** — Isabella de Bourgogne was buried at Ghent "ten Galileen" — the Galilee being a chapel attached to the main church, possibly St. Bavo's Cathedral or another Ghent church. Verification via the Ghent Dominican chronicles or the St. Bavo burial register (if surviving).
- **Insular Chamber of Accounts register 1515** — Archives départementales du Nord (Lille), Chambre des Comptes series B — the original 20 April 1515 appointment register should survive.

### 3.5 Lodewijk IV van Vlaenderen / Louis of Praet (d. 1555)

**Vredius reference:** Vol 2, Tabula XIX, p. 387. Full tomb inscription in the middle of the chancel of Aalter church. Grimarez on the same page.

**Identification:** Son of Lodewijk III and Isabella of Burgundy. **Knight of the Golden Fleece (1531).** Lord of Praet, Woestyne, Elverdinghe, Vlamertinghe, Spiete, and de Mersch. Senior Burgundian-Habsburg officer under Charles V: Councillor, Upper-Chamberlain, Chief of Finances, High-Bailiff of Bruges and of the Bruges Vrije; at his death, Governor and Captain of Flanders. Grand Bailly of Ghent from 20 April 1515 (per the misplaced entry in §3.4). Died 1555. Buried at Aalter beside his father and grandfather.

**Original text — Aalter tomb inscription (Vol 2 p. 387, Middle Dutch):**

> *Desumptum videtur ex eorum Epitaphio, quod, in medio chori de Altere, in tumba è lapide cæruleo, refert Gaillard hoc sensu: Inden middel vanden choor/ inde groote blauwe tombe/ light Mijn-Heere/ M'her LODEWYC van Vlaendren/ Rudder vander Ordre vanden Gulden Vliese/ Heere van Praet/ vanden lande vander Woestyne/ Elverdijnghe/ Vlamertijnghe/ Spiete/ ende vander Mersch/ Raed/ Upper-camerlinck/ Chief vande Financien van de K.M. Carolus den V. ende sijnen Hoogh-Bailliu van Brugghe/ ende van 't Brughsche Vrije/ te sijnen overlijden Gouverneur ende Capitain van Vlaendren/ die starf 1555.*

**Original text — Grimarez, French (p. 387):**

> *D. Grimarezius: Meßire LOVYS de Flandres, Chevalier de l'ordre de la Toison d'or, Seig. de Praet, du pays de la Woestine, de Woeste, d'Elverdinghe, & Vlamertinghe, d'Espier, & de Mersch, Conseillier, second Chambellan, & Chief des Finances de l'Empereur Charles V. & son Bailly de la ville de Bruges, espousa Dame IOSSINE de Praet, heritiere de Moerkercke; laquelle mourut en l'an 1555, gisent à Haltre.*

**Translation (Middle Dutch tomb):** *It seems to be taken from their epitaph, which in the middle of the chancel of Altere [Aalter], in a great blue-stone tomb, Gaillard reports thus: In the middle of the choir, in the great blue tomb, lies My Lord, Sir LODEWYC van Vlaendren, Knight of the Order of the Golden Fleece, Lord of Praet, of the land of Woestyne, Elverdinghe, Vlamertinghe, Spiete, and of the Mersch; Councillor, Upper-Chamberlain, Chief of the Finances of His Imperial Majesty Charles V, and his High-Bailiff of Bruges and of the Brugges Vrije; at his death Governor and Captain of Flanders; who died 1555.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **Grimarez's date for Jossine is erroneous.** Grimarez writes "Dame IOSSINE de Praet… *laquelle mourut en l'an 1555, gisent à Haltre*." The "1555" applies grammatically to Jossine (*laquelle* = feminine relative pronoun), but the tomb inscription itself — quoted in full on the same page — gives Jossine's death as 10 December 1546. See §3.6. Grimarez has apparently conflated the husband's year with the wife's. The Middle Dutch tomb is primary; Grimarez is secondary and erroneous on this point.
- **Full titulature significance.** Lodewijk IV's titles represent the peak of the Van Vlaenderen-Praet line's political reach: Knight of the Golden Fleece at the highest Burgundian-Habsburg chivalric rank; Chief of Finances of Charles V positions him at the center of imperial fiscal administration; Governor and Captain of Flanders at his death was the senior crown office in the county itself. No later Van Vlaenderen figure in the surviving documented lines came close to this rank.
- **Spiete / Mersch.** Two lordships added to the Praet portfolio at or after Lodewijk IV's marriage: *Spiete* (Espier, in French form per Grimarez — possibly Oostwinkel-adjacent but location uncertain) and *de Mersch* (also uncertain; possibly a polder lordship in the Franc).
- **Cross-reference to Wikipedia's Louis of Praet.** The modern Wikipedia biography identifies this figure as Louis of Praet, Knight of the Golden Fleece (inducted 1531 at the Tournai chapter), with full curriculum vitae through his Stadtholdership of Holland and Zeeland (1544–1546).

### 3.6 Jossine van Praet (d. 10 December 1546)

**Vredius reference:** Vol 2, Tabula XIX, p. 387. Aalter tomb inscription continuation (same stone as Lodewijk IV).

Daughter of Charles van Praet, Heer van Moerkercke, and Paschijne van Halewijn (daughter of Charles van Halewijn, Heer van Uytkercke). **Heiress of the original van Praet baronial family** — meaning the Van Vlaenderen line held the Praet lordship by descent from Louis Friese (granted 1373 by Louis de Male) and by marriage to the last Praet baronial heiress. ### 3.7 Jan II van Vlaenderen (d. 10 December 1545, *avant son pere*)

**Vredius reference:** Vol 2, Tabula XIX, p. 388. Dedicated entry with Aalter tomb and Grimarez gloss.

**Identification:** Son of Lodewijk IV and Jossine van Praet. Heer van Woestyne, Elverdinghe, and Vlamertinghe. Married **Jacqueline (Iaquemijne) de Bourgogne** (daughter of Adolf, Heer van Beveren and Veere, Knight of the Golden Fleece) in **1540**. Died **10 December 1545** — ten years before his father, without issue (*starf sonder generatie*). **The legitimate Praet male line ended with his death, a decade before the 1555 Lodewijk IV himself died.**

**Original text — Aalter tomb inscription (Vol 2 p. 388, Middle Dutch):**

> *Gaillardus locum sepulturæ illorum designat in monumento paterno Halterensi, his verbis: Inde selve tombe light Jo. IAN van Vlaendren/ Heere van Woestine/ Elverdinghe/ ende Vlamertinghe/ fil. mijns Heeren Lodewijc/ Heere van Praet/ ende van Vrauw' Josijne voorseyt/ die starf 1545. den 10. December; hy hadde ghetrauwt Vrauw' IAQVELINE van Bourgongnen/ fil. M'her Adolf/ Heere van Bevere/ starf sonder generatie.*

**Original text — Grimarez, French (Vol 2 p. 388):**

> *Ioannes à Flandria, Dominus de Praet &c.; Iacoba Burgundica, filia Adolphi, Equitis. Commemoratur in genealogia præcedente. D. Grimarezius: IEHAN de Flandres, Seig. de Praet, & de la Woestine, Elverdinghe, &c. espousa 1540, Dame IAQVELINE de Bourgongne, fille de Meßire Adolf, Chevalier de la Toison d'Or, Seigneur de Bevere, vander Vere &c. Il mourut, sans generation, avant son pere, en l'an 1545. le 10. de Decembre: gist à Haltere.*

**Translation (tomb):** *Gaillard records the place of their burial in the paternal monument at Aalter, in these words: In the same tomb lies Jonkheer JAN van Vlaendren, Lord of Woestine, Elverdinghe, and Vlamertinghe, son of my Lord Lodewijk, Lord of Praet, and of Lady Josyne aforesaid, who died 1545, the 10th of December; he had married Lady IAQVELINE of Burgundy, daughter of Sir Adolf, Lord of Bevere, died without issue.*

*Grimarez: Sir Jean of Flanders, Lord of Praet and of Woestine, Elverdinghe, &c., married in 1540 Lady Iaqueline of Burgundy, daughter of Sir Adolf, Knight of the Golden Fleece, Lord of Bevere, of Vere, &c. He died, without issue, before his father, in the year 1545, the 10th of December: lies at Haltere.*

**Evidentiary notes:**

- **"Avant son pere, en l'an 1545" — the key phrase.** Grimarez's formulation is explicit: Jan II predeceased his father Lodewijk IV. Lodewijk IV lived on until 1555 (see §3.5); the legitimate Praet male line was therefore extinct from 1545 onwards, with the senior line surviving only through Lodewijk IV's own lifetime as a child-less widower for his last decade.
- **Extinction point dated to 10 December 1545.** This is the single most important date for the Praet line's research role in the project: the legitimate line is extinct from this date. Any later "Van Vlaenderen" figure cannot descend from the senior Praet line; must come from (a) an earlier cadet branch (e.g., Josse de Flandre's line via Martina van Moerkerke, documented to ~1592 per Buylaert), (b) another Louis II bastard line (Victor, Robert of Elverdinghe's natural sons, Jan sans terre), (c) a different origin entirely.
- **Jaquemijne de Bourgogne's second marriage.** Cross-referenced in Vol 2 p. 388 continuation: she remarried Jan, Heer van Cruijningen, and died "*van haer laetste kint*" ("of her last child") at Beveren in 1556 — i.e., in childbirth with her last child by her second husband. Her Veere epitaph reads to that effect.
- **Day of death — note the 10 December coincidence:** Mother Jossine died 10 December 1546; son Jan died 10 December 1545. Exactly one year apart to the day. Too specific to be a scribal error for only one of them; plausibly a coincidence of anniversary, possibly a commemorative date deliberately chosen (second anniversary masses were a common medieval/early-modern devotional pattern). The tomb text and Grimarez agree on both dates.

**Site placement:** `PraetLineageDossierPage` Generation 6 prose — the Pass 1 patch added the "predeceased father by ten years" context and cross-linked the Grimarez *avant son pere* phrasing.

**Research leads:**

- **Veere epitaph of Jaquemijne de Bourgogne** — referenced via FMG [895]. Full text should be sought in the Zeeuws Archives (Middelburg) if the Veere church monument survives.
- **Adolf van Bourgogne's own genealogy.** Adolf of Beveren, Knight of the Golden Fleece — his full biography and descendants (including Jaquemijne and her siblings Margareta and Cornelius — see Vol 2 p. 381 and adjacent pages) would require a separate dossier thread.

## 6. Cross-cutting notes

### 6.1 The three-tier Count-Louis distinction in Vredius's own Table XVI

Vredius's Table XVI arranges three sequential Counts Louis and their respective bastard children:

- **"Nivernensis"** = Louis, Count of Nevers (d. 1322). Father of Louis of Crécy. Never Count of Flanders.
- **"Cressiacensis"** = Louis of Crécy = Louis I of Flanders (d. 1346). Son of Nivernensis. Father of Louis of Male.
- **"Maleanus"** = Louis of Male = Louis II of Flanders (d. 1384). Son of Cressiacensis. Father of Victor, Louis Friese, Jan sans terre, Robert of Elverdinghe, Louis Haze, Isabella, and others.

Modern genealogy typically refers only to "Louis I of Flanders" (Cressiacensis) and "Louis II of Flanders" (Maleanus), omitting the Nivernensis generation. This simplification obscures the cohort Vredius preserves:

- Isabella of Somergem is a Nivernensis bastard — Victor's **great-aunt**, not his aunt.
- The knight-brothers (Tristram, Ruflard, Lancelot, Percevall, Guy) are Cressiacensis bastards — Victor's **uncles** in the bastard line.
- Baudouin's sons Willem, Loyken, Ghielynken are Cressiacensis grandsons (if the 1409 reading is correct) — Victor's **first cousins** in the bastard line.

This cohort structure is important when assessing descent candidates: a surname-bearing 15th- or 16th-century Van Vlaenderen could in principle descend from any of the three cohorts, though record density falls sharply in the two older ones.

### 6.2 The Ghent partition court registers

Vredius's standard citation formula "*In actis curiae partitionum Gandensium, Ex regist. part.* f.56" identifies a specific Ghent administrative register containing estate partitions and related inheritance records. This register is the primary archival substrate for the Victor charter nucleus.

- **Modern identification:** Probably corresponds to the Rijksarchief Gent *Jaarregisters van de Keure* (Schepenbank van Keure registers) or the related *Staten van Goed* series for the City of Ghent.
- **Status:** Not independently verified by the project as of April 2026. Verification of f.56 against the original register would constitute direct primary-source validation of the Vredius transcription layer.

### 6.3 Bethune (1900) *Epitaphes* as transcription layer

The Oostburg tomb (Jacqueline de Wilde and Joos van Vlaenderen) and certain Aalter tomb passages reach Vredius via Bethune's later 19th-century collection work. The FMG MedLands footnotes [848]–[849] attribute these directly to Bethune. Bethune is print-only (not digitised); held at KBR Brussels (Royal Library of Belgium) and Ghent University Library.

### 6.4 De l'Espinoy (1631) as prior genealogical authority

Philippe de l'Espinoy, *Recherche des antiquitez et noblesse de Flandres* (Douai, 1631), is cited repeatedly by Vredius — particularly for the 1383 Drincham grant (§4.1) and the Praet territorial holdings (§1.3.2). De l'Espinoy pre-dates Vredius by twelve years. The *Recherche* is digitised on Gallica (BnF). Where Vredius quotes Espinoy verbatim, both sources should be cited.

### 6.5 Grimarez (*D. Grimarezius*) as an internal Vredius attribution

Many 16th- and 17th-century French-language passages in Vredius are attributed to "D. Grimarezius" — a manuscript source held by a contemporary of Vredius's and lent to him for transcription. The Grimarez manuscript survives only through these Vredius extracts. It is the single source for certain date/day-of-death readings that conflict with tomb-inscription readings in the same page (see Lodewijk II and Lodewijk III at §3.2, §3.3).

## 7. Downgraded and deferred

This section records candidates examined during the validation pass and either downgraded or deferred for archival verification. They do not currently appear on public dossier pages, but are preserved here so the research basis remains auditable.

### 7.1 The 1469-vs-1409 Baudouin guardianship date problem

See §1.2.2 for the full evidence. Vredius prints 1469 (impossible given Baudouin's 1350s–1360s activity); supplement uses 1409. Archival verification at Rijksarchief Gent (Jaarregisters van de Keure, c. 1400–1470) required before this entry can be finalised on the site.

### 7.2 Jan van Ursele filiation — unestablished in Vredius

See §5.1. The 1489 Drossard-of-Mechelen appointment is firmly attested; the hypothesis that Jan van Ursele descends from a Louis-II-bastard line is not supported by the Vredius placement (Tabula XIX, ducal household — not under any Maleanus bastard header). Retained as a research target only.

### 7.3 Louis Haze's single attested son Renaud — no documented continuation

See §1.3.1. Renaud (Renault) de Flandres, Lord of le Vacke, attested in the charter of 24 February 1397 only. No further descent located. Line appears to terminate in one generation.

### 7.4 Isabella van Vlaanderen / Oosteeklo Abbey — regional context only

A 14th-century Isabella van Vlaanderen is associated with the Oosteeklo Abbey foundation in certain local histories. She is not a Louis-de-Male bastard; the association is chronologically and genealogically separate from the Van Vlaenderen surname research. Retained in regional background but not a progenitor candidate.

### 7.5 The Louis I Cressiacensis cohort — downgraded, not invalidated

See §1.2. The full cohort (Guido of Wedelghem, Baudouin + sons, Robert of Ghent, the knight-brothers, Maria, Catharina, Clara of Angelendale) is fully attested in Vredius. The cohort is downgraded for two reasons:

- Time gap: Louis of Crécy died 1346, fifty years before Louis of Male. Any surviving surname-bearing descendants would need one more generation of continuity than the Maleanus lines, which sharply reduces the probability of surviving to the modern parish-record period.
- Descent patterns: most lines exit via female marriage (Maria → Poelvoorde; Catharina → Boudins / vanden Eeckhoute; Guido → Margriete → Messem; Isabella of Somergem → Mirabello / Huerle), carry the surname out of the Van Vlaenderen line in one generation.

Retained in this document for completeness. Not given dedicated site real estate on the "probability of living surname-bearing descent" filter that governs site emphasis.
