I. Primary Sources and Finding Aids

Charter Compilations and Early Printed Authorities

Primary SourceVredius, Olivarius (Olivier de Wree)(1635–1643)
Genealogia Comitum Flandriae a Balduino Ferreo usque ad Philippum IV. Hisp. Regem (Pars Prima & Pars Secunda), with the Sigilla Comitum Flandriae
Bruges: J.B. & Lucas Kerchovius, 1635–1643. Multiple imprints; the relevant volumes are detailed in the note.
The single most important early-modern authority for this project, consulted across the several physical volumes that together make up Vredius's corpus on the Counts of Flanders (per-volume title-page dates verified 27 May 2026): (1) Pars Secunda, the 'Probationes' — standalone 1635 imprint plus the 1643 alt-edition bound with the combined 'a Balduino' set — is the load-bearing volume, reproducing verbatim the Ghent partition-court registers, charters, and tomb inscriptions of the comital bastard cohort. Tabula XVI covers the Louis I de Cressy bastards (pp. 267–275) and the Louis II de Male bastards (pp. 275–289), including the three Victor charters (pp. 285–287); Tabula XIX covers the 16th-century Praet generation (Lodewijk IV, Jossine van Praet, Jan II, pp. 387–388). The project's canonical distillation works from this volume, read at 300 DPI in April 2026. (2) Pars Prima (1642) supplies Vredius's narrative genealogy from Baldwin Iron-Arm to Philip IV, framing the Probationes. (3) Sigilla Comitum Flandriae (Bruges: J.B. Kerchovius, 1639) is the sigillographic companion reproducing the comital seals and diplomatic inscriptions, relevant to the heraldic and sigillographic context in which the surname emerges — it is the comital seal-book, not a source for bastard-line heraldry. FMG MedLands was consulted earlier as a tertiary pointer to these pages; that footnote crosswalk is kept in the project's research files.

Primary SourceEspinoy, Philippe de l'(1631)
Recherche des antiquitez et noblesse de Flandres
Douai. Livre 2, Chap. XXXI.
The principal authority for Victor's parentage and for the Praet territorial grants. Espinoy was a Flemish herald and his work is a foundational reference for Flemish noble genealogy, though he does not always cite his own sources. Quoted verbatim at many points by Vredius; this project consults Espinoy both directly (where the print is available via Gallica) and through the Vredius transmission.
Free access (Gallica / BnF)
Primary SourceBethune, J.B. de(1900)
Epitaphes et monuments des eglises de la Flandre au XVIe siecle
Bruges. Third part, p.356.
Transcriptions of epitaphs from Flemish churches. The Oostborch epitaph for Jacqueline de Wilde and Josse van Vlaenderen is preserved here, providing the only direct evidence of Lodewyc van Vlaenderen's family through the physical burial record. The project also cites p. 233 (Elverdinge) for the epitaph evidence bearing on the identity of Robrecht van Vlaenderen's mother.
Primary SourceVerschelde, Karel(1867)
Geschiedenis van Middelburg in Vlaenderen
Brugge: De Zuttere(-Van Kersschaver), 1867. ~272 pp. + plates, with a names/places index and a Bewysstukken document appendix.
A Bruges local history of Middelburg-in-Flanders by the architect-historian Karel Verschelde (1842-1881). Its document appendix transcribes the 1441 Cadzand koopbrief as Bewysstukken N° 1 — the deed carrying the bare-with-honorific 'mher Victoors van Vlaenderen' attestation used in the project's register analysis of how recognized nobles of the cohort appear in fiscal and conveyancing records.
Primary SourceGailliard, J[ean-Jacques](1857-1864)

Bruges: chez l'auteur J. Gailliard, rue de la Bride. Multi-volume series; the volume consulted on FamilySearch film 1116147 carries the date 1859.
Genealogical and magisterial repertory of Bruges and the Brugse Vrije, transcribing tomb inscriptions and family records. Primary Historical Chronicles

Primary SourceAnon. (ed. Vorsterman, Willem)(1531)
Excellente Cronike van Vlaenderen
Antwerp: Willem Vorsterman, 1531. Large folio edition.
Chronicle of the foresters and counts of Flanders, from Liederic de Buc (621 AD) to the late 15th century. Printed in 1531 as the first edition of a chronicle tradition preserved in seven related 15th-century manuscripts. The heraldic shields displayed on the Name page are from this chronicle tradition. Referenced for context on the world the Van Vlaenderen family inhabited.
Free access (Utrecht University Repository — 1531 Vorsterman edition)
Primary SourceDespars, Nicolaes(1840-1842 [written c. 1590])
Cronijcke van den lande ende graefscepe van Vlaenderen (405-1492)
Ed. J. De Jonghe. Brugge, 1840-1842. 4 vols.
The late-sixteenth-century Middle Dutch chronicle of Flanders by the Bruges patrician Nicolaes Despars, in the nineteenth-century De Jonghe edition. Cited site-wide: the narrative spine of the Maleani bastard-cohort attestations (the Vol. III master enumeration and the dated career entries for dHaze, Hector, Robrecht, Victor and their brothers) and of the project's Despars compendium. Pirenne's caution that the Cronijcke is 'hardly more than a translation' of Meyer's Annales is carried in the project's source-criticism; the two witnesses are read together rather than counted as independent.
Primary SourceMeyer, Jacob (Iacobus Meyerus Baliolanus)(1561)
Commentarii sive Annales rerum Flandricarum libri septendecim
Antwerp: Joannes Steelsius, 1561. Posthumous expanded edition; the 1531 Annalium Flandricarum libri XVII was the first.
The humanist Latin chronicle of Flanders by Jacob Meyer of Bailleul (1491-1552), in the canonical 1561 citation edition. Cited on dossier pages for the Latin-register attestations of the Maleani cohort — p. 200 ('Ludovicum qui & Aza'; the bastard-son roster including 'Hectorem Vorholtium', also at p. 163). Per Pirenne, the prior chronicle witness behind Despars's Cronijcke.

Recueil des chroniques de Flandre / Corpus Chronicorum Flandriae
4 vols. Brussels: Commission royale d'histoire / M. Hayez, 1837-1865.
The nineteenth-century critical edition of the Flemish chronicle corpus. Primary SourceSanderus, Antonius(1735)
Historia Comitum Flandriae, Pars Prima: Flandria Ethnica — a primo consulatu Caj. Jul. Caesaris usque ad Clodovaeum primum Francorum regem Christianum, per DLIV annos
In: Flandria illustrata, sive Provinciae ac Comitatus hujus descriptio, Tomus Tertius (1735 edition). Hagae-Comitum [The Hague] / Brussels: Carolus & Joannes-Baptista De Vos Fratres, MDCCXXXV.
Sanderus's narrative history of the territory that became the County of Flanders, from Caesar's first consulate (59 BC) through the reign of Clovis I (d. 511). A distinct work from his better-known Flandria Illustrata (see sanderus-1641), though the two were bundled together in the 1735 Hague/Brussels expanded reissue, which is the edition consulted here. Pairs with Pars Secunda (Flandria Christiana) below. Useful for the pre-comital geographic and political framing of Flanders, and for Sanderus's handling of the legendary first-forester figure Liederic de Buc.
Primary SourceSanderus, Antonius(1735)
Historia Comitum Flandriae, Pars Secunda: Flandria Christiana — a Clodoveo primo Francorum rege Christiano, usque ad annum DCCLXVII, Pipini Francorum Regis XVI
In: Flandria illustrata, sive Provinciae ac Comitatus hujus descriptio, Tomus Tertius (1735 edition). Hagae-Comitum [The Hague] / Brussels: Carolus & Joannes-Baptista De Vos Fratres, MDCCXXXV.
The continuation of Sanderus's narrative history of Flanders, running from Clovis I through the sixteenth regnal year of Pippin (767 AD). Covers the Merovingian and early Carolingian period of Flemish prehistory, ending just before the formal establishment of the hereditary county under Baldwin Iron-Arm in the 860s. Bundled with Pars Prima and with Flandria Illustrata in the 1735 Hague/Brussels Tomus Tertius.

Primary SourceVan Vlaenderen American-line primary documents (1854–1913)(1854–1913)
Primary records for Charles Louis Van Vlaenderen (b. Bassevelde 1854, d. Covington KY 1913), emigrant ancestor of the American branch
Belgian civil registers (Bassevelde, Oost-Vlaanderen) and American civil, court, and ecclesiastical records (Indiana, Ohio, Kentucky). Specific records consulted include: Bassevelde geboorteakte no. 52, registered 14 June 1854 for a birth on 13 June 1854 (the father declared the child the following morning; son of Engelbertus van Vlaenderen and Marie Catherine De Clerck); Allen County (Indiana) naturalization declaration, 5 October 1880; Lucas County (Ohio) Probate Court naturalization affidavit, 5 April 1884; Kenton County (Kentucky) death certificate no. 2793 and probate file, October 1913.
The documentary chain anchoring the project's direct American line to the Bassevelde civil register. The Bassevelde 1854 geboorteakte is the critical primary-source join between the European parish-record dossier and the American branch that prompted the Lions of Flanders project; all subsequent American records cohere with the Belgian origin despite orthographic drift (Van Vlaenderen, Van Vlanderen, Van Flandern, Van Vanderen, Van Flanderen) across U.S. records. Finding AidGysseling, Maurits (†) & Debrabandere, Frans(1999)
Persoonsnamen in de Vier Ambachten, 14e en 15e eeuw
Handelingen van de Koninklijke Commissie voor Toponymie en Dialectologie (KCTD / HCTD), vol. 71 (1999), pp. 491-588.
Posthumous edition, prepared by Debrabandere, of Gysseling's (1919-1997) note-card corpus of personal names attested in the Vier Ambachten (Assenede, Boekhoute, Axel, Hulst) during the 14th and 15th centuries. The corpus was compiled chronologically from schepenbank and related records; Gysseling's original 600 note cards yielded approximately 3000 attestations of around 1000 distinct individuals (983 men, 13 women). The first large-scale, unbiased genetic study of historical extra-pair paternity (EPP) rates in a Western European population, combining Y-chromosomal data from contemporary Flemish men with genealogical and surname records to estimate the historical non-paternity event rate. Methodologically and geographically the most directly relevant prior work for the Van Vlaenderen project: it establishes the empirical baseline against which any apparent surname-paternity discrepancy in the Flemish Y-DNA record must be assessed. Genetic GenealogyLarmuseau, M.H.D., Delorme, P., Germain, P., Vanderheyden, N., Gilissen, A., Van Geystelen, A., Cassiman, J.-J., & Decorte, R.(2014)
Genetic genealogy reveals true Y haplogroup of House of Bourbon contradicting recent identification of the presumed remains of two French Kings
European Journal of Human Genetics, 22(6), 681–687. doi:10.1038/ejhg.2013.211.
The methodological touchstone for this project's Y-DNA work. Larmuseau and co-authors collected samples from three living documented Bourbon paternal descendants and demonstrated that the reconstructed 'true' Bourbon Y-chromosomal variant (R-Z381*, a subclade of R-U106) is incompatible with the published Y-STR profiles of the alleged 'Louis XVI blood' and 'Henri IV head' samples — inverting the earlier identifications of those relics. The paper is the canonical demonstration of why living-descendant Y-chromosomal data, not isolated historical samples, are the necessary anchor for any claim of identification across many generations. This is the exact logic underpinning the collaboration with Pieter van Vlaanderen: the question is not whether any single historical sample can be recovered, but whether the Y-profiles of multiple independently-documented living descendants of the project's hypothesised founders converge on a single ancestral signature.

A Flanders-scale study of Y-chromosomal diversity across six Flemish communities at the time of surname establishment (13th–17th c.), finding high Y-chromosomal diversity and low within-community relatedness among citizens carrying distinct surnames. Directly relevant to the project's founder-count question: the baseline expectation for a generic toponymic surname in the Low Countries is that bearers within a single community should NOT share a recent common paternal ancestor. If a Flemish Y-DNA project of Van Vlaenderen bearers were to find substantially more within-name relatedness than Larmuseau's communal-scale baseline predicts, that would be positive evidence for a bastard-line or office-holder-founder model over a generic toponymic one.

E. Heraldry and Sigillography

Aristocratic seal ownership in twelfth-century Flanders: A world in between
Preprint. Academia.edu, 2021. University of Namur. Forthcoming in peer-reviewed publication.
A sigillographic study of noble seal usage in the County of Flanders, 1071–1200. Establishes that territorial designations in the elite Flemish milieu functioned as political and dynastic language, not mere geographic labels. Documents Philip of Alsace placing the Flemish lion on the comital seal from 1163; confirms Michael II of Harnes (castellan of Cassel, constable of Flanders) as a Dover Recognitio sealer; and confirms the Praet lordship as an established noble entity from at least c.1190–1200. Provides contextual depth for the Van Vlaenderen surname hypothesis.

G. Collaborator-Compiled Family Registers

MeetjeslandVan Vlaanderen, Pieter Antheunisz(2020)
Stamreeks van Vlaanderen — De Zeeuwse Tak-2, ~1500 tot 2020
Privately compiled family register. Author resident in Schoorl, The Netherlands.
A private, collaborator-compiled register of the 'Zeeuwse Tak' (Zeeland branch) of the Van Vlaenderen family from approximately 1500 through 2020, prepared by Pieter Antheunisz van Vlaanderen in Schoorl. The register documents the Zeeland line descending from Laureys Arentsz van Vlaenderen, schoolmaster at Ritthem (attested 1588, d. late 1601), whose signature appears on the cover alongside the compiler's own. This compilation is the documentary basis for the ongoing Big Y-700 testing collaboration between Pieter and this project: the reconstructed Zeeland lineage serves as the reference pedigree against which Y-chromosomal results can be interpreted. Cited here to declare the provenance of the DNA collaboration transparently; as a privately-compiled family register, it is treated as a collaborator-provided secondary source rather than a peer-reviewed scholarly publication.
